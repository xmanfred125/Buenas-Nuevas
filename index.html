Abba Post · October 20, 2026Abba Post · 20 de octubre de 2026

On the evening of October 9, a mob of roughly 200 armed fanatics arrived in vehicles at the Divine Love Retreat Centre in Jalalkhali, Nadia district, West Bengal, where the Annual Santhali Mega Bible Convention Retreat was underway. Witnesses said the attackers carried bamboo sticks and bricks, hurled stones at the gathering, shouted anti-Christian slogans, and vandalized church property. Several believers were injured in the assault.

The convention had drawn more than 1,900 participants on its opening day, with organizers expecting some 2,500 over the four-day program. Instead, the retreat was forced to end early as the violence scattered the crowd. It was a gathering of Santhali tribal Christians — one of India's indigenous communities with a growing evangelical presence.

The Catholic Bishops' Conference of India condemned the attack on October 12, saying a false “forced conversion” narrative broadcast over loudspeakers had deliberately incited the crowd, and appealed to Home Minister Amit Shah to intervene. An opposition fact-finding team led by MP Sagarika Ghose reported that Christians in the region face pressure to abandon their faith, denial of burial rights, and vandalized churches.

Independent watchdog India Hate Lab documented at least 60 targeted hate crimes against Christians in West Bengal between May and September 2026. For the Santhali believers who came seeking four days of worship and teaching, the stones cut the retreat short — but the church in West Bengal has learned to count the cost, and to gather again.

Testigos dijeron que los atacantes llevaban palos de bambú y ladrillos, lanzaron piedras contra la multitud, gritaron consignas anticristianas y vandalizaron la propiedad de la iglesia. Varios creyentes resultaron heridos en el ataque.

La convención había reunido a más de 1.900 participantes en su día inaugural, y los organizadores esperaban unos 2.500 a lo largo del programa de cuatro días. En cambio, el retiro debió terminar antes de tiempo cuando la violencia dispersó a la multitud. Era una reunión de cristianos tribales santalíes, una de las comunidades indígenas de India con creciente presencia evangélica.

La Conferencia de Obispos Católicos de India condenó el ataque el 12 de octubre, afirmando que una falsa narrativa de “conversión forzada” difundida por altavoces había incitado deliberadamente a la multitud, y pidió la intervención del ministro del Interior Amit Shah. Un equipo opositor de investigación encabezado por la diputada Sagarika Ghose informó que los cristianos de la región enfrentan presión para abandonar su fe, negación del derecho a entierro e iglesias vandalizadas.

He was buried two days later, leaving behind a wife and two children. His killing followed the June execution of Baptist pastor William and fellow Christian Nam Ceu, who had been arrested in May.

The violence against churches continued into October. Days later, International Christian Concern reported that the military-aligned Shanni Nationalities Army had been using Nant Mun Baptist Church in Mohnyin Township for military activities — including holding a graduation ceremony on church grounds, with dancing and drinking inside the sanctuary itself.

Reuters reports Washington has held two rounds of talks with the junta in about a month — in Naypyidaw in September and Tokyo in October. The U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom has recommended redesignating Myanmar a Country of Particular Concern for its severe violations of religious freedom.

Myanmar's Baptists — among the country's largest Christian communities, concentrated in Chin and Kachin states — have borne a heavy share of the junta's campaign since the 2021 coup. Churches burned, pastors killed, sanctuaries turned into barracks: the pattern is unmistakable. The global church is called to remember them, by name, in prayer.

PRRI Survey Finds White Evangelicals Alone in Seeing Empathy as DangerousEncuesta de PRRI: solo los evangélicos blancos ven la empatía como peligrosa

The 17th annual American Values Survey found white evangelicals are the only major religious group where a majority sees empathy as potentially dangerous — and the only one where a majority supports internment camps for undocumented immigrants.La 17.ª Encuesta Anual de Valores Estadounidenses halló que los evangélicos blancos son el único grupo religioso importante donde la mayoría ve la empatía como potencialmente peligrosa, y el único donde la mayoría apoya campos de internamiento para inmigrantes indocumentados.

Abba Post · October 20, 2026Abba Post · 20 de octubre de 2026

Only 48 percent of white evangelical Protestants agree that “empathy is a foundational moral value that helps build a healthy society,” while 54 percent say “empathy can become dangerous when it leads people away from building a society guided by God's truth.” That finding — from the 17th annual American Values Survey, released October 20 by the Public Religion Research Institute in partnership with the Brookings Institution — makes white evangelicals the only major religious group in America where skepticism of empathy is the majority view.

The survey of 5,200 adults, conducted August 20 through September 7, also traced a hardening of views on immigration. Republican support for a citizenship pathway for undocumented immigrants has fallen from 53 percent in 2013 to 37 percent today, while support for deporting all undocumented immigrants rose from 32 to 50 percent. White evangelicals (53 percent) were the only religious group in which a majority supports placing undocumented immigrants in internment camps while awaiting deportation.

PRRI president Robert P. Jones said the results reveal a widening divide: “As Christian nationalist leaders attack the sentiment of empathy, a disturbing 'empathy gap' has opened, separating Republicans and white evangelicals from most other Americans.”

For pastors and theologians, the numbers pose a searching question. Scripture commands love of neighbor and compassion for the stranger dozens of times — yet a majority of one large Christian constituency now views empathy itself with suspicion. The gap is not only political; it is pastoral.

The survey lands in a season when American evangelicalism is already examining its public witness. Whether empathy is recovered as a Christian virtue — or surrendered as a liability — may shape the church's voice for a generation.

Solo el 48 por ciento de los protestantes evangélicos blancos está de acuerdo en que “la empatía es un valor moral fundamental que ayuda a construir una sociedad sana”, mientras que el 54 por ciento dice que “la empatía puede volverse peligrosa cuando aleja a las personas de construir una sociedad guiada por la verdad de Dios”. Este hallazgo —de la 17.ª Encuesta Anual de Valores Estadounidenses, publicada el 20 de octubre por el Public Religion Research Institute en alianza con la Brookings Institution— convierte a los evangélicos blancos en el único grupo religioso importante de EE. UU. donde el escepticismo hacia la empatía es la opinión mayoritaria.

La encuesta, realizada a 5.200 adultos entre el 20 de agosto y el 7 de septiembre, también registró un endurecimiento de las posturas sobre inmigración. El apoyo republicano a una vía de ciudadanía para inmigrantes indocumentados cayó del 53 por ciento en 2013 al 37 por ciento actual, mientras que el apoyo a deportar a todos los indocumentados subió del 32 al 50 por ciento. Los evangélicos blancos (53 por ciento) fueron el único grupo religioso donde la mayoría apoya recluir a los inmigrantes indocumentados en campos de internamiento mientras esperan la deportación.

El presidente de PRRI, Robert P. Jones, dijo que los resultados revelan una brecha creciente: “Mientras líderes nacionalistas cristianos atacan el sentimiento de la empatía, se ha abierto una inquietante 'brecha de empatía' que separa a republicanos y evangélicos blancos de la mayoría de los estadounidenses”.

Para pastores y teólogos, las cifras plantean una pregunta incómoda. Las Escrituras ordenan decenas de veces amar al prójimo y tener compasión del extranjero; sin embargo, la mayoría de un amplio sector cristiano hoy mira la empatía con sospecha. La brecha no es solo política; es pastoral.

La encuesta llega en una temporada en que el evangelicalismo estadounidense ya examina su testimonio público. Que la empatía se recupere como virtud cristiana —o se entregue como un lastre— puede marcar la voz de la iglesia por una generación.

Christian rapper Hulvey made the hardest call of his career on October 16: canceling a sold-out concert in Amsterdam roughly an hour before showtime. High demand had moved the show within the Melkweg complex to Club Annie across the street — and when his team arrived, they found the room's walls covered in what the artist called “extremely, extremely graphic” pornographic images. ChatGPT Fields 70 Million Religion-Related Requests a Week, OpenAI ReportsChatGPT recibe 70 millones de consultas religiosas por semana, informa OpenAI

Abba Post · October 20, 2026Abba Post · 20 de octubre de 2026

People are bringing their faith questions to artificial intelligence in staggering numbers. OpenAI's October 2026 report, “Faith, Community & AI,” found that ChatGPT fields more than 70 million religion-related requests every week. From September 2025 to September 2026, about 45 percent of those concerned Islam, 34 percent Christianity, 4 percent Hinduism, 2 percent Judaism, and 1 percent Buddhism.

The breakdown is revealing: roughly 22 million requests per week concern the study of religious texts — “People ask ChatGPT to explain difficult passages, find relevant commentaries, and help them understand how their traditions approach particular questions” — while 16 million concern practices and holidays and another 16 million concern beliefs, meaning, and spiritual guidance. Nearly half of Christianity-related messages were about Bible passages, and 13 percent more about worship, sermons, or church practices.

The report concludes that people worldwide are “using AI as a tool to support their religious lives,” and notes that faith leaders themselves use AI to prepare teaching and organize their communities. The findings were reported October 20 by the Christian Post's Leonardo Blair.

The numbers raise questions the church cannot ignore: who is discipling the millions asking a chatbot about Scripture? AI can explain a difficult passage — but it cannot shepherd a soul. For pastors and parents, the report is both a wake-up call and an invitation: meet the seekers where they are, with the depth no algorithm can fake.

Las personas están llevando sus preguntas de fe a la inteligencia artificial en cifras asombrosas. Entre septiembre de 2025 y septiembre de 2026, cerca del 45 por ciento se refirió al islam, el 34 por ciento al cristianismo, el 4 por ciento al hinduismo, el 2 por ciento al judaísmo y el 1 por ciento al budismo.

El desglose es revelador: unas 22 millones de consultas semanales se refieren al estudio de textos religiosos —“la gente le pide a ChatGPT que explique pasajes difíciles, que encuentre comentarios relevantes y que les ayude a entender cómo sus tradiciones abordan ciertas preguntas”—, mientras 16 millones se refieren a prácticas y festividades y otros 16 millones a creencias, sentido y guía espiritual. Casi la mitad de los mensajes relacionados con el cristianismo trataban sobre pasajes bíblicos, y un 13 por ciento más sobre adoración, sermones o prácticas eclesiales.

Abba Post · October 21, 2026Abba Post · 21 de octubre de 2026

An Essex church is taking its fight for street preaching to court. Bread of Life Community Church, backed by the Christian Legal Centre, will appeal a Community Protection Notice issued by Colchester City Council at a hearing beginning Thursday, October 22 — with a second day set for October 29. The church calls it an “unprecedented use of public-order powers” against an entire congregation rather than individual preachers.

The notice, issued in March 2026 after a warning in November 2025, bans amplified equipment and what the council terms “engaging or intimidating behaviour” — with no geographical limit, no volume threshold, and no expiry date. Breaching it is a criminal offence. The church says the order “goes beyond concerns about amplification and seeks to regulate the content of Christian preaching, including references to traditional biblical teachings” such as hell.

Public backing has been significant: more than 27,000 people signed a CitizenGo petition demanding the notice be withdrawn, delivered to council chief executive Pamela Donnelly on October 14.

The case will test how far British public-order law can reach into the content of preaching. For churches across the UK watching closely, the question is simple: if a council can criminalize a church's message on the street, where does it stop?

Una iglesia de Essex llevará su lucha por la predicación callejera a los tribunales. Bread of Life Community Church, respaldada por el Christian Legal Centre, apelará un Aviso de Protección Comunitaria emitido por el ayuntamiento de Colchester en una audiencia que comienza el jueves 22 de octubre, con una segunda jornada prevista para el 29 de octubre. La iglesia lo califica como un “uso sin precedentes de los poderes de orden público” contra una congregación entera, en lugar de predicadores individuales.

El aviso, emitido en marzo de 2026 tras una advertencia en noviembre de 2025, prohíbe equipos de amplificación y lo que el ayuntamiento llama “conducta insistente o intimidatoria”, sin límite geográfico, sin umbral de volumen y sin fecha de vencimiento. Incumplirlo es un delito penal. La iglesia afirma que la orden “va más allá de la preocupación por la amplificación y busca regular el contenido de la predicación cristiana, incluidas referencias a enseñanzas bíblicas tradicionales” como el infierno.

El respaldo público ha sido significativo: más de 27.000 personas firmaron una petición de CitizenGo exigiendo retirar el aviso, entregada a la directora ejecutiva del ayuntamiento, Pamela Donnelly, el 14 de octubre.

El caso pondrá a prueba hasta dónde puede llegar la ley británica de orden público en el contenido de la predicación. Para las iglesias de todo el Reino Unido que siguen el proceso de cerca, la pregunta es simple: si un ayuntamiento puede criminalizar el mensaje de una iglesia en la calle, ¿dónde se detiene?

Two months after a mob left him unconscious in the street, 17-year-old Ikhlaq Masih is healing — and forgiving. The Pakistani Christian teen, severely beaten on August 16 in Nasrat Colony, Sheikhupura, told International Christian Concern that about two-thirds of his wounds have healed, and that he has forgiven the men who attacked him.

The assault began when Masih tried to protect a girl from harassment on her way to church. Ali Shah — son of an influential local man — and his gang hurled religious slurs at him, calling him “chura,” a derogatory term for Christians in Pakistan, then ambushed him alone with pistols, iron rods, and motorcycle chains. “My Christian faith was what led me to stand up for that girl and protect my sister in Christ,” Masih said. “I pray that God will show them the light.”

Eight people were originally charged; three had charges dismissed and the case against five continues — but the family fears the influence of Shah's family, and Shah has reportedly threatened to kill Masih if they pursue the case. Masih has moved to a new village and started a new job.

A teenager who defended a sister in Christ, paid for it with his body, and answers with forgiveness: the global church should know Ikhlaq Masih's name — and pray his courage, and his mercy, are not in vain.

The battle for eastern Ukraine is now threatening the churches that feed it. As Ukrainian forces fight to hold Kramatorsk and Sloviansk — the Donetsk “fortress belt” — new curfews in the east are choking off Christian aid delivery, Mission Network News reported October 20.

The two cities serve as critical distribution hubs for the Slavic Gospel Association (SGA): the transportation hub lets supplies reach central churches, which distribute them to surrounding villages. “In the absence of a centralized system, it becomes very complicated to get out to these villages,” SGA's Eric Mock told the outlet. Under Ukraine's Operation Vivaldi, the army is trying to protect supply routes and retake territory — but “as Ukraine begins to push back and fight against the Russian army, these frontline villages are caught in the middle,” Mock said.

Civilians trapped on the Russian-controlled side — “single mothers, elderly people, impoverished families” — have almost no access to supplemental church aid and “really have no capacity to leave.”

When the front line moves, it is the church network that absorbs the shock: the same congregations that worship on Sunday become the warehouses, kitchens, and lifelines of Monday. Pray for the drivers, the pastors, and the villages caught in the middle.

La batalla por el este de Ucrania ahora amenaza a las iglesias que lo alimentan. Mientras las fuerzas ucranianas luchan por mantener Kramatorsk y Sloviansk —el “cinturón fortaleza” de Donetsk—, nuevos toques de queda en el este están asfixiando la entrega de ayuda cristiana, informó Mission Network News el 20 de octubre.

Las dos ciudades sirven como centros críticos de distribución para la Slavic Gospel Association (SGA): el nodo de transporte permite que los suministros lleguen a iglesias centrales, que los distribuyen a las aldeas aledañas. “Sin un sistema centralizado, se vuelve muy complicado llegar a estas aldeas”, dijo Eric Mock, de SGA, al medio. Another container of Bibles is bound for Hong Kong. Mission Cry is sending a fresh shipment of free Bibles and Christian books to the city, seeing an urgent window as China tightens its influence — and as some partners fear Christian materials could soon be blocked from import altogether, Mission Network News reported October 19.
