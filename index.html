Evangelist Franklin Graham sat down this week in North Wilkesboro, North Carolina, with one of the most powerful religion officials in the Communist world: Nguyễn Đình Khang, Vietnam's Minister of Ethnic and Religious Affairs and a member of the Communist Party of Vietnam's Central Committee.

The meeting, announced Thursday by the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association, brought together Khang, other high-ranking Vietnamese officials and three Vietnamese evangelical church leaders. According to the association, Minister Khang committed that 'the government of Vietnam, at all levels of administration, is committed to promote religious freedom,' and described 'open channels' — digital platforms, dialogues and letters — through which religious minorities can raise their concerns.

The Vietnamese delegation spent two days touring the ministries Graham leads. On Tuesday they visited the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association's headquarters in Charlotte, and on Wednesday they saw Samaritan's Purse's international relief staging center in North Wilkesboro — the ministry Graham also leads as CEO and which has operated in Vietnam for more than 30 years.

Graham, who has visited Vietnam seven times in 25 years and preached there on three occasions, has watched the evangelical church grow from a tiny minority. Today evangelical Christians make up an estimated 1 to 2 percent of Vietnam's population of more than 100 million.

Vietnam remains a country where Christians face significant restrictions, and believers will be watching whether the minister's words translate into concrete freedoms on the ground. For now, Graham's camp framed the encounter as a hopeful step: a Communist government minister personally pledging religious freedom to one of the world's most prominent evangelists, on American soil.

Abba Post · October 13, 2026Abba Post · 13 de octubre de 2026

One of the largest churches in American Methodism may be headed for the exit. The Church Council of Highland Park United Methodist Church in Dallas voted unanimously this week to call a churchwide vote, October 25–26, on whether to sever all ties with The United Methodist Church.

The ballot question reads: 'Shall Highland Park Methodist Church… sever all of its ties, affiliations, and connections to The United Methodist Church?' The council is recommending a 'Yes' vote to its more than 15,000 members — the second-largest congregation in the denomination.

At the root of the rupture is a lawsuit filed August 10 by the UMC's Horizon Texas Conference over changes the church made to its governing documents — articles of incorporation and bylaws amended in November 2022. The conference contends the amendments improperly removed denominational and congregational oversight. Highland Park counters that it was the conference and Bishop Rubén Saenz Jr. who broke the church's right of self-governance.

The tension was on full display at a town hall on October 11 that drew some 800 members. Council chair Caroline Nowlin framed the question plainly: 'Knowing what we know now, should our church remain part of The United Methodist Church?'

If members vote to leave, Highland Park would become the highest-profile departure in the ongoing realignment of American Methodism, following years of disaffiliation battles over doctrine and church governance. For now, the council is betting that a clear majority of its flock believes the congregation's future lies outside the denomination.

Una de las iglesias más grandes del metodismo estadounidense podría estar a punto de salir. La pregunta en la boleta dice: '¿Deberá la Iglesia Metodista de Highland Park… romper todos sus lazos, afiliaciones y conexiones con la Iglesia Metodista Unida?' El consejo recomienda votar 'Sí' a sus más de 15.000 miembros —la segunda congregación más grande de la denominación.

En la raíz de la ruptura hay una demanda presentada el 10 de agosto por la Conferencia Horizon Texas de la UMC por los cambios que la iglesia hizo a sus documentos de gobierno —acta constitutiva y estatutos enmendados en noviembre de 2022. La conferencia sostiene que las enmiendas eliminaron indebidamente la supervisión denominacional y congregacional. Por ahora, el consejo apuesta a que una clara mayoría de su grey cree que el futuro de la congregación está fuera de la denominación.

R. Scott Pace, 51, was formally inaugurated Tuesday as the seventh president of Southeastern Baptist Theological Seminary in Wake Forest, North Carolina, in a ceremony at Binkley Chapel.

Pace succeeds Danny Akin, who retired July 31 after a 22-year tenure. He assumed office August 1 after serving as the seminary's provost, and his installation marks another chapter in a generational leadership shift underway across the Southern Baptist Convention's six seminaries.

The seminary, one of the largest in the SBC, receives about 16 percent of its budget from the Cooperative Program — the convention's unified missions funding — tying its future closely to the churches it serves.

The task before Pace is significant: to keep Southeastern anchored to its confessional and missional convictions while training pastors and missionaries for a rapidly changing religious landscape. His inauguration signals continuity with the seminary's conservative resurgence roots — and a younger hand on the wheel.

More Christians face persecution today than at any point in the modern record. Open Doors released its 2026 World Watch List on Wednesday, reporting that 388 million Christians — more than one in seven worldwide — now face high levels of persecution or discrimination, an increase of more than eight million from the previous year.

The annual list, whose methodology is audited by the International Institute for Religious Freedom and covers the twelve months ending September 30, 2025, ranks the 50 countries where following Christ costs the most. The top ten: North Korea, Somalia, Yemen, Sudan, Eritrea, Syria, Nigeria, Pakistan, Libya and Iran.

Nigeria remains the deadliest country in the world to be a Christian: 3,490 of the 4,849 faith-related killings documented globally occurred there, up from 3,100 the prior year. Extreme violence is concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, with 14 countries from the region on the list. Somalia ranked second for the fourth consecutive year, while Syria recorded its highest persecution score ever — the largest single-year jump since the current methodology began in 2014.

The report is not without glimmers of hope. Violence against Christians fell by 20 percent in Bangladesh, and in Malaysia a court reopened the 2017 kidnapping case of Pastor Raymond Koh — acknowledging police involvement and ordering $7.4 million in compensation to his family.

Behind every figure is a congregation, a pastor, a family. The list's release each October is a summons to the global church: to pray, to speak, and to remember that the fastest-growing church in the world is also, in many places, the most persecuted.

La lista anual, cuya metodología es auditada por el Instituto Internacional para la Libertad Religiosa y cubre los doce meses que terminaron el 30 de septiembre de 2025, clasifica los 50 países donde seguir a Cristo cuesta más. Los diez primeros: Corea del Norte, Somalia, Yemen, Sudán, Eritrea, Siria, Nigeria, Pakistán, Libia e Irán.

La violencia extrema se concentra en el África subsahariana, con 14 países de la región en la lista. Abba Post · October 7, 2026Abba Post · 7 de octubre de 2026

Pakistan's Supreme Court has upheld the death sentence of Qaiser Ayub, a 49-year-old Christian computer science graduate from Lahore, convicted under Section 295-C of the Penal Code for blasphemous material published on a website registered in his name.

The three-member bench, headed by Chief Justice Yahya Afridi, announced its decision on October 7. The case has wound through the courts for more than a decade: Qaiser was arrested in 2014 and his brother Amoon Ayub in 2015; a trial court sentenced both men to death in 2018; the Lahore High Court upheld those sentences in 2022. The Supreme Court acquitted Amoon, 46, and ordered his release.

The brothers' legal team, working with the American Center for Law and Justice, says the Supreme Court hearing was postponed 13 times since 2025. Arguments were heard on July 6 and the judgment was reserved until October 7. The lawyers now plan to file a revision petition, calling the ruling 'a dangerous precedent.'

A self-described 'blasphemy business group' leader, Rao Abdur Raheem, celebrated the verdict as the first time the Supreme Court 'barrier has been crossed in 40 years' since Section 295-C was added to the penal code.

For Pakistan's small Christian minority, the ruling is a stark reminder that the blasphemy laws remain a sword hanging over every believer — and a call to prayer for Qaiser's family and his legal team as they pursue the last legal avenue available to them.

La Corte Suprema de Pakistán confirmó la sentencia de muerte de Qaiser Ayub, un cristiano de 49 años graduado en informática de Lahore, condenado bajo la Sección 295-C del Código Penal por material blasfemo publicado en un sitio web registrado a su nombre.

El tribunal de tres jueces, presidido por el juez presidente Yahya Afridi, anunció su decisión el 7 de octubre. El caso se ha arrastrado por los tribunales durante más de una década: Qaiser fue arrestado en 2014 y su hermano Amoon Ayub en 2015; un tribunal de primera instancia sentenció a ambos a muerte en 2018; el Tribunal Superior de Lahore confirmó esas sentencias en 2022. Two pastors who fled Sudan's war now face death threats in the very refugee camp where they sought safety. Drij Haruon Khamis and Adel Idris, leaders of the Darfur Christian Council, are being threatened at the Gorom Refugee Settlement in South Sudan, 26 kilometers southwest of Juba, by Muslim extremists encouraged by the camp's mosque imam.

Tensions spiked September 25, when a man lingered for about 30 minutes outside Pastor Khamis' home at 9 p.m., appearing to monitor him with a mobile phone. The pastors believe he was sent by the same extremists who attacked Khamis at his church site in May, injuring his leg. Police arrested the man, who remains in custody.

Sudan ranks fourth on Open Doors' 2026 World Watch List — and for these two pastors, persecution followed them into exile.

After nearly a decade as one of the leading storytellers at the intersection of sports and faith, Jason Romano is stepping into a new calling. Romano spent 17 years at ESPN before joining Sports Spectrum in February 2017, where he built its podcast into a flagship platform for athletes sharing how faith shapes their careers. Pennsylvania Court: Judges Can't Curb Parents' Right to Guide a Child's FaithTribunal de Pensilvania: los jueces no pueden recortar el derecho de los padres a guiar la fe de sus hijos

Pennsylvania's Superior Court vacated a custody provision barring parents from influencing their child's religious life, ruling that directing a child's faith upbringing is a constitutionally protected parental right limitable only on proof of grave harm.El Tribunal Superior de Pensilvania anuló una disposición de custodia que impedía a los padres influir en la vida religiosa de su hijo, fallando que dirigir la formación de fe de un niño es un derecho parental protegido por la Constitución, limitable solo ante prueba de daño grave.

Abba Post · October 14, 2026Abba Post · 14 de octubre de 2026

A Pennsylvania appeals court has drawn a bright constitutional line around one of parenting's most sacred duties: guiding a child's faith. In Lajara v. McMenamin, decided October 14, the Superior Court of Pennsylvania vacated a custody-order provision that had let a child decline religious or faith-based events at age 14 — and had barred both parents from 'influencing, restricting, or limiting' the child's religious participation.

The striking detail: neither parent had asked for the restriction. Writing for the panel, Judge Brandon Neuman — joined by Judges Jack Panella and Carolyn Nichols — held that parents enjoy a constitutionally protected right to direct their children's religious upbringing under the First and Fourteenth Amendments, one that courts may limit only upon a showing of a 'grave' or 'substantial threat' of physical or emotional harm.

The court expressly rejected the idea that judges may preemptively curb a child's religious exposure simply because interfaith or co-parenting conflict might arise someday. Anticipated clashes, the opinion reasoned, cannot justify present restrictions.

The case involved a mother's appeal concerning her 11-year-old child, but its reasoning reaches every household of faith navigating custody disputes: the state does not get to edit a parent's discipleship of their own child on the basis of hypothetical future friction.

For Christian parents, the decision is a precedent-level affirmation — faith formation at home is not a privilege courts grant, but a right the Constitution protects.

Un tribunal de apelaciones de Pensilvania trazó una clara línea constitucional alrededor de uno de los deberes más sagrados de la crianza: guiar la fe de un hijo. En el caso Lajara v. McMenamin, decidido el 14 de octubre, el Tribunal Superior de Pensilvania anuló una disposición de custodia que permitía a un menor rechazar eventos religiosos o basados en la fe a los 14 años —y que prohibía a ambos padres 'influir, restringir o limitar' la participación religiosa del niño.

El detalle llamativo: ninguno de los padres había pedido la restricción. En nombre del panel, el juez Brandon Neuman —junto a los jueces Jack Panella y Carolyn Nichols— sostuvo que los padres gozan del derecho constitucionalmente protegido de dirigir la formación religiosa de sus hijos bajo la Primera y la Decimocuarta Enmienda, uno que los tribunales solo pueden limitar ante una demostración de amenaza 'grave' o 'sustancial' de daño físico o emocional.

El tribunal rechazó expresamente la idea de que los jueces puedan recortar preventivamente la exposición religiosa de un niño simplemente porque un conflicto interreligioso o de coparentalidad podría surgir algún día. Los choques anticipados, razonó la opinión, no pueden justificar restricciones presentes.

El caso involucraba la apelación de una madre respecto a su hijo de 11 años, pero su razonamiento alcanza a cada hogar de fe que navega disputas de custodia: el Estado no puede editar el discipulado que un padre hace de su propio hijo basándose en fricciones futuras hipotéticas.

Para los padres cristianos, la decisión es una afirmación con valor de precedente: la formación en la fe en el hogar no es un privilegio que otorgan los tribunales, sino un derecho que protege la Constitución.

Beneath the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem, archaeologists have uncovered something that may be a road to Calvary itself. A recently published study led by Prof. Francesca Romana Stasolla of Sapienza University of Rome, director of the excavation, describes a paved stone roadway — roughly ten by twelve feet, with a wide, low step bordered by larger blocks — found beneath a Crusader-period walkway.

The road's curvature and steep gradient suggest it rose toward the southwest — possibly leading to the area used for public executions. The broad step rules out cart traffic for Roman construction work, and Roman crucifixions required open, accessible roads: public display was part of the punishment.

Earlier excavations at the site had already found traces of cultivated plants consistent with the Gospel of John's account of a garden and a tomb. The new findings add tangible, peer-reviewed texture to the setting of the Passion narrative.

The researchers are careful: the connection to Christ's route remains hypothetical. But for believers, the stones offer something precious — the growing archaeological case that the traditional site of the crucifixion and resurrection sits on ground Jesus himself would have walked.

Bajo la iglesia del Santo Sepulcro en Jerusalén, los arqueólogos han descubierto algo que podría ser el camino al Calvario mismo. Un estudio publicado recientemente, liderado por la profesora Francesca Romana Stasolla de la Universidad Sapienza de Roma, directora de la excavación, describe un camino empedrado —de unos diez por doce pies, con un escalón ancho y bajo bordeado por bloques más grandes— hallado bajo una pasarela del período cruzado.

La curvatura del camino y su pronunciada pendiente sugieren que ascendía hacia el suroeste —posiblemente hacia la zona usada para ejecuciones públicas. El amplio escalón descarta el tráfico de carros para obras romanas de construcción, y las crucifixiones romanas requerían caminos abiertos y accesibles: la exhibición pública era parte del castigo.

Excavaciones anteriores en el sitio ya habían hallado rastros de plantas cultivadas consistentes con el relato del Evangelio de Juan sobre un huerto y un sepulcro. Los nuevos hallazgos añaden textura tangible y revisada por pares al escenario de la narrativa de la Pasión.
