U.S. Regulator Confirms Nine Major Banks Closed Christian Groups' Accounts Because of Their Faith

Abba Post · October 14, 2026Abba Post · 14 de octubre de 2026

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency released a 47-page report on Wednesday, October 14, 2026, titled “Debanked: Guaranteeing Fair Banking for All Americans.” After reviewing nearly 100,000 complaints and tens of thousands of internal bank records from 2020 through September 2025, the federal regulator concluded that all nine major banks it supervises had policies that “required, encouraged, or otherwise influenced” decisions to deny or limit financial services based on political or religious beliefs — most often citing “reputation risk.”

The banks named in the report are JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citibank, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank, Capital One, PNC, TD Bank and BMO. Among the documented cases, JPMorgan's payment arm WePay shut down a religious nonprofit after the Southern Poverty Law Center labeled it a hate group, while the bank's merchant-screening terms flagged phrases such as “MAGA,” “Make America Great” and “America First.” Wells Fargo, the report says, pressed a payment processor to cut off a religious nonprofit over its views on marriage.

Jeremy Tedesco, senior counsel at Alliance Defending Freedom, said Christians who reported being debanked were routinely told they were imagining it: “Now we know definitively that it is happening. It was happening at scale.” The investigation, covered in depth by CBN News on October 16 under the headline “Investigation of 9 Banks Finds Irrefutable Evidence that Religious Groups Were Targeted,” was also reported by the New York Post from the OCC release.

For believers, the finding touches a biblical theme as old as the early church: pressure on livelihoods as a tool of pressure on faith. Scripture reminds Christians to render to Caesar what is Caesar's while giving to God what is God's — and to pray for those in authority. The report's prayer-worthy outcome would be simple: a banking system in which faith no longer counts as a reputational risk.
