Cristian Mungiu's "Fjord" — the Palme d'Or drama about a devout Christian family under the scrutiny of Norway's child-welfare system — comes home on digital platforms Tuesday, November 24, 2026, via NEON, priced at $19.99 sale, according to When To Stream's weekly preview.

The film stars Sebastian Stan as Mihai Gheorghiu, a devout Romanian Christian, and Renate Reinsve as his Norwegian wife Lisbet; they move with their five children to her remote coastal hometown in Norway. The story is loosely inspired by the real Bodnariu family case, whose children were taken into Norwegian child-welfare care; in the film the family is renamed Gheorghiu.

"Fjord" won the Palme d'Or at the 2026 Cannes Film Festival — Mungiu's second Palme d'Or — and opened in U.S. theaters on October 9 (limited) and October 23, 2026. The film runs about 146 minutes, and Stan and Reinsve are receiving Oscar buzz for their performances, according to When To Stream.

In an interview published November 22, Mungiu said he did not want the film to tell viewers which side to take; the family's beliefs are a source of friction but are not treated as a reason to dismiss the family. For Christian audiences, that restraint is part of what makes the film worth watching: a story about a believing family, told without caricature.

For believers who follow how faith is portrayed on screen, "Fjord" arrives at a moment of renewed attention to the subject — a Cannes winner, a digital release within reach of any living room, and a director who insists the audience decide for itself.
