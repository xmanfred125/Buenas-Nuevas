German Evangelical Alliance expands its 1946 confession of guilt toward the Jewish peopleLa Alianza Evangélica de Alemania amplía su confesión de culpa de 1946 hacia el pueblo judío

Eighty years after its first confession, Germany's evangelical alliance has asked the Jewish people for forgiveness, admitting its leaders welcomed Hitler's rise and defended anti-Jewish measures with arguments drawn from the Bible.Ochenta años después de su primera confesión, la alianza evangélica de Alemania ha pedido perdón al pueblo judío, admitiendo que sus líderes recibieron con agrado el ascenso de Hitler y defendieron medidas antijudías con argumentos sacados de la Biblia.

The Evangelical Alliance in Germany has asked the Jewish people for forgiveness, admitting that its leaders welcomed Adolf Hitler's rise to power in 1933 and later defended anti-Jewish measures with arguments drawn from the Bible. The alliance, known by its German initials EAD, which links about 900 local alliances and more than 380 affiliated organizations, released the statement on November 9 as an expansion of a confession of guilt it first issued in 1946.

Three alliance leaders read the text aloud at a memorial devotional service at the EAD's Berlin office, which stands beside the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe. The 1946 confession conceded that the alliance had not always recognized the guilt of its own people, and the new statement goes further, naming the failure plainly: "We confess that our fathers became guilty before God and before the Jewish people."

The date was deliberate. November 9 is the anniversary of the 1938 pogrom known in German as the Reichspogromnacht, and the release came 80 years after the alliance's first confession, which appeared in a commemorative publication marking the centenary of the Evangelical Alliance, founded in London in 1846. The alliance's periodical, Allianzblatt, did not condemn the exclusion and early persecution of Jews with the clarity required, the statement says.

The statement also commits the alliance to protecting Jewish people today. German police recorded 6,548 antisemitic crimes in 2025, a record and a 5 percent increase over the previous year, according to the Federal Criminal Police Office. For German evangelicals, the confession is both repentance for the past and a pledge for the present: to stand with their Jewish neighbors and to name antisemitism clearly whenever it appears.

La confesión de 1946 reconocía que la alianza no siempre había reconocido la culpa de su propio pueblo, y la nueva declaración va más lejos, nombrando el fracaso con claridad: «Confesamos que nuestros padres se hicieron culpables ante Dios y ante el pueblo judío».

La fecha fue deliberada. El periódico de la alianza, Allianzblatt, no condenó la exclusión y la persecución temprana de los judíos con la claridad requerida, señala la declaración.

La declaración también compromete a la alianza a proteger hoy a los judíos. La policía alemana registró 6.548 delitos antisemitas en 2025, un récord y un aumento del 5 por ciento respecto al año anterior, según la Oficina Federal de Policía Criminal. Para los evangélicos alemanes, la confesión es a la vez arrepentimiento por el pasado y compromiso para el presente: estar junto a sus vecinos judíos y denunciar el antisemitismo con claridad cada vez que aparezca.
