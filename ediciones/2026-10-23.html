First-Ever African World Assemblies of God Congress Draws 10,000 Leaders to AccraEl primer Congreso Mundial de las Asambleas de Dios en África reúne a 10.000 líderes en Acra

More than 10,000 delegates from nearly 200 countries gathered in Accra, Ghana, October 14–16, for the first World Assemblies of God Congress ever held in Africa — rallying around a vision of one million churches by 2033.Más de 10.000 delegados de casi 200 países se reunieron en Acra, Ghana, del 14 al 16 de octubre, en el primer Congreso Mundial de las Asambleas de Dios celebrado en África, con una visión de un millón de iglesias para 2033.

Abba Post · October 22, 2026Abba Post · 22 de octubre de 2026

Accra, Ghana, has just hosted a moment of historic proportions for the global Pentecostal movement. From October 14 to 16, the 10th Triennial World Congress of the World Assemblies of God Fellowship gathered more than 10,000 delegates from nearly 200 countries — the first time in the gathering's 50-year history it has ever been held in Africa.

The theme, "Commissioned: One Mandate, Every Nation" (Matthew 28:18–20), centered on the MM33 Mandate: the fellowship's vision to plant one million Assemblies of God churches worldwide by 2033, marking 2,000 years since Christ's resurrection. The movement builds on more than 451,000 churches and about 89 million adherents across 180-plus nations — which AG Ghana describes as the largest Protestant denomination in the world, with roughly 20 million believers in Africa alone.

Daytime leadership sessions filled the Accra International Conference Centre, while evening revival rallies packed the Accra Sports Stadium, where gospel artists including Sinach, Joe Mettle and Dunsin Oyekan joined a 500-voice congress choir. Speakers included Ghana's President John Dramani Mahama, WAGF Chairman Rev. Dominic Yeo of Singapore, host Rev. Stephen Wengam, General Superintendent of AG Ghana, Bishop Dag Heward-Mills, and WAGF Vice Chairman Rev. Doug Clay.

"It has never come to Africa before. This is the first time in 50 years. And this is Africa's time of revival," Dr. Kwadwo Boateng Bempah, Assistant Superintendent of AG Ghana, told Joy FM. The gathering was open to Christians of all denominations, underlining a shared mission beyond one movement's borders.

In follow-up coverage this week, Rev. Wengam expressed gratitude for the successful hosting, and local organizing chairman Rev. Nelson Awintia was commended for the organization of the event. For Abba Post readers, Accra's message rings clear: the future of the global Church is being written not only in the West, but in Africa — and Africa is ready.

Acra, Ghana, acaba de ser sede de un momento de proporciones históricas para el movimiento pentecostal mundial. Del 14 al 16 de octubre, el 10.º Congreso Mundial Trienal de la Fraternidad Mundial de las Asambleas de Dios reunió a más de 10.000 delegados de casi 200 países: la primera vez en los 50 años de historia de la reunión que se celebra en África.

El tema, "Comisionados: un mandato, cada nación" (Mateo 28:18-20), giró en torno al Mandato MM33: la visión de la fraternidad de plantar un millón de iglesias de las Asambleas de Dios en todo el mundo para 2033, al cumplirse 2.000 años de la resurrección de Cristo. El movimiento se apoya en más de 451.000 iglesias y unos 89 millones de adherentes en más de 180 naciones, lo que las AD de Ghana describen como la denominación protestante más grande del mundo, con unos 20 millones de creyentes solo en África.

Las sesiones de liderazgo del día llenaron el Centro Internacional de Conferencias de Acra, mientras que las concentraciones nocturnas de avivamiento abarrotaron el Estadio Deportivo de Acra, donde artistas gospel como Sinach, Joe Mettle y Dunsin Oyekan se unieron a un coro de 500 voces del congreso. Entre los oradores estuvieron el presidente de Ghana, John Dramani Mahama, el presidente de la WAGF, el Rev. Dominic Yeo de Singapur, el anfitrión Rev. Stephen Wengam, superintendente general de las AD de Ghana, el obispo Dag Heward-Mills y el vicepresidente de la WAGF, el Rev. Doug Clay.

"Nunca había venido a África. Es la primera vez en 50 años. Y este es el tiempo del avivamiento de África", dijo a Joy FM el Dr. Kwadwo Boateng Bempah, superintendente asistente de las AD de Ghana. La reunión estuvo abierta a cristianos de todas las denominaciones, subrayando una misión compartida más allá de las fronteras de un solo movimiento.

En la cobertura de seguimiento de esta semana, el Rev. Wengam expresó su gratitud por la exitosa organización, y el presidente del comité organizador local, el Rev. Nelson Awintia, fue felicitado por su trabajo. Para los lectores de Abba Post, el mensaje de Acra suena claro: el futuro de la Iglesia mundial no se está escribiendo solo en Occidente, sino en África, y África está lista.

Christianity Today Publishes, Pulls, Then Restores Controversial Article on Elisabeth ElliotChristianity Today publica, retira y luego restaura un polémico artículo sobre Elisabeth Elliot

A Christianity Today essay speculating about the late missionary's inner life was published October 15, taken down amid fierce backlash, and restored the next day — fueling debate about the magazine's turbulent leadership.Un ensayo de Christianity Today que especulaba sobre la vida interior de la fallecida misionera se publicó el 15 de octubre, se retiró en medio de una fuerte reacción y se restauró al día siguiente, avivando el debate sobre el liderazgo turbulento de la revista.

Abba Post · October 18, 2026Abba Post · 18 de octubre de 2026

On Thursday, October 15, Christianity Today published an essay by Lucy S. R. Austen — author of a 2024 CT Book Award-winning biography of Elisabeth Elliot — titled "What We Know — and Cannot Know — About Elisabeth Elliot's Sexuality." The piece speculated, based on a 2020 oral-history interview with the late Virginia Ramey Mollenkott recalling an alleged private encounter from 1975, that the late missionary widow may have experienced same-sex attraction. The reaction was swift and fierce — and by the end of the day the article had been removed, its page returning only the whimsical 404 message: "Oops! Looks like this page is formless and void."

The piece returned on Friday, October 16, with the text unchanged but "properly framed," according to a new editor's note from Erik Petrik. Petrik had begun that very week as Christianity Today's new chief content officer and editor-in-chief — announced October 2, succeeding Marvin Olasky, who published his farewell on October 16. Petrik explained that the article had not been reviewed by key content-team members before publication "in the midst of internal leadership transitions."

The backlash had been unsparing. Indiana Lt. Gov. Micah Beckwith wrote that "Christianity Today has officially lost its ever-living mind"; Denny Burk of the Council on Biblical Manhood and Womanhood called the speculation "outlandish" with "exactly zero evidence"; and megachurch pastor Josh Howerton posted simply: "Farewell CT." Austen herself told The Roys Report she had received "no direct explanation" for the removal, calling the episode "surprising and disappointing."

The episode is part of a wider season of turbulence at the flagship evangelical magazine: six editors-in-chief in under seven years, a staff reduction of roughly 20 percent and an editorial budget cut of 40 percent this October, and the resignation of deputy editor Bonnie Kristian. What should have been a quiet leadership handoff has become another public reckoning over the magazine's direction.

For Christian readers, the deeper question is pastoral as much as journalistic: how do we honor the memory of servants of God who can no longer speak for themselves? The consensus from believers across traditions this week was clear — that even beloved figures deserve better than speculation about their private hearts, decades after their deaths.

The meeting, held the week before the report's publication on October 20, brought together workers who share the gospel in cities, universities, and Muslim-background contexts across Europe and the Balkans, a region where public proclamation often faces legal and cultural headwinds.

The conference follows earlier editions — including one in Sarajevo in November 2025 that drew 150 participants from 13 countries — and reflects a growing appetite for coordinated evangelism among European believers. Rather than competing strategies, the emphasis was connection: evangelists sharing what works in difficult soil.

The Sarajevo gathering also fits a wider pattern of evangelical coordination across Europe this October. The Fellowship of European Evangelical Theologians met in Golem, Albania, from October 10 to 13 on the theme "Faithful in Times of Turmoil"; the Nordic Refugee conference gathered in Kauniainen, Finland, October 15–17; and TWR Europe brought together some 100 leaders from 21 countries at its 2026 partner conference.

Abba Post could not independently confirm the Sarajevo attendance figures from a second source; the account above is reported by Evangelical Focus. But the pattern it describes is consistent with a continent where evangelism is shifting from lone pioneers to networked, prayerful cooperation — and where Bosnia, a land that has known deep wounds, is becoming a meeting place for the harvest.

On Sunday, October 18, around 11 a.m. — the hour when Sunday services are normally held — a Russian guided air-to-ground bomb struck the "Good News" church building in Kramatorsk, northwest of Donetsk in eastern Ukraine. According to Evangelical Focus, the damage to the well-known Pentecostal congregation, led by Pastor Viktor Pavenko, who has served the city for more than 30 years, is described as total.

No casualties occurred, for one reason only: the building had not been used since Saturday morning, following a warning of a high risk of a targeted strike. The bomb fell on an empty house of prayer — a providential warning that likely saved lives.

The congregation belongs to the Ukrainian Church of Christians of the Evangelical Faith, and since the 2022 invasion it had become a humanitarian centre: distributing food, water and hot meals and offering psychological and spiritual support to thousands. The church also runs a bread ministry for the poor and the elderly. This was the second attack on the building: in June 2026, three shells damaged its roof and shattered its windows.

The strike fits a wider and grim pattern. According to the producers of the documentary project "A Faith Under Siege," which also reported the Kramatorsk bombing, at least 58 priests and pastors have been killed and more than 700 churches damaged or destroyed in Ukraine since 2022. Abba Post notes that this second account comes from an advocacy media project rather than a conventional newsroom, and reports it here as corroboration of the main facts.

When the congregation returns, it will return to rubble — but the church was never the bricks. In Kramatorsk, the church is the people who fed the hungry, warmed the cold, and prayed through the bombs. Pray for Pastor Pavenko and his flock as they worship on: homeless, but not forsaken.

La congregación pertenece a la Iglesia Ucraniana de Cristianos de la Fe Evangélica, y desde la invasión de 2022 se había convertido en un centro humanitario: distribuyendo alimentos, agua y comidas calientes y ofreciendo apoyo psicológico y espiritual a miles de personas. Abba Post señala que esta segunda cuenta proviene de un proyecto mediático de defensa de derechos más que de una redacción convencional, y la reporta aquí como corroboración de los hechos principales.

Christian Group's "Underground Railroad" Frees 1,500 Sudanese Slaves in a YearEl «ferrocarril subterráneo» de una organización cristiana libera a 1.500 esclavos sudaneses en un año

Christian Solidarity International says it freed 1,500 Sudanese slaves in the past year through its underground network — pushing its three-decade total past 100,000 liberated.Christian Solidarity International dice haber liberado a 1.500 esclavos sudaneses en el último año a través de su red subterránea, elevando su total de tres décadas a más de 100.000 liberados.

Abba Post · October 19, 2026Abba Post · 19 de octubre de 2026

Christian Solidarity International (CSI) has announced that it helped free 1,500 Sudanese slaves in the past year, bringing its three-decade total of liberations to more than 100,000 — with CSI's own 2025 accounting placing the 30-year figure at over 160,000. The news was reported by CBN News in the week of October 19.

Joel Veldkamp, CSI's head of international communications, told CBN News that Christians and followers of indigenous religions in South Sudan have been hardest hit. During the 1983–2005 civil war, he said, the northern Muslim government used slavery as a "weapon of war": horsemen and camel raiders swept down on villages, killings and brutality were used to break spirits before enslavement, and people were forced into cargo trains bound for the north.

Nearly 20 years after the war's end, many remain "stuck" in captivity — including a second generation born into slavery, children who have never known a day of freedom. Tens of thousands are estimated to still be in bondage.

CSI works through an underground network of Arab "slave retrievers" — a modern underground railroad. For about $250 per person, the organization exchanges a cattle vaccine plus a survival kit, a dairy goat, food, health care and safe passage home, continuing at a pace of roughly 1,500 liberations a year.

The ministry's credibility is not only its own claim: CSI holds a four-star rating from Charity Navigator (2025) and a MinistryWatch "Give with Confidence" award. For Abba Post readers, the math is as simple as it is convicting: the price of one person's freedom is less than a family's monthly groceries. Pray for the retrievers, for the freed, and for the tens of thousands still waiting.

Christian Solidarity International (CSI) anunció que ayudó a liberar a 1.500 esclavos sudaneses en el último año, elevando su total de tres décadas a más de 100.000 liberaciones, y la contabilidad propia de CSI para 2025 sitúa la cifra de 30 años en más de 160.000. La noticia fue reportada por CBN News en la semana del 19 de octubre.

Joel Veldkamp, director de comunicaciones internacionales de CSI, dijo a CBN News que los cristianos y los seguidores de religiones indígenas en Sudán del Sur han sido los más afectados. Durante la guerra civil de 1983-2005, dijo, el gobierno musulmán del norte usó la esclavitud como un "arma de guerra": jinetes y camelleros caían sobre las aldeas, y los asesinatos y la brutalidad servían para quebrar los espíritus antes de la esclavitud, mientras las personas eran forzadas a subir a trenes de carga rumbo al norte.

Casi 20 años después del fin de la guerra, muchos siguen "atrapados" en cautiverio, incluida una segunda generación nacida en la esclavitud: niños que nunca han conocido un día de libertad. Se estima que decenas de miles aún permanecen en servidumbre.

CSI trabaja a través de una red subterránea de "rescatadores de esclavos" árabes: un moderno ferrocarril subterráneo. Por unos $250 por persona, la organización intercambia una vacuna para ganado más un kit de supervivencia, una cabra lechera, alimentos, atención médica y un pasaje seguro a casa, manteniendo un ritmo de unas 1.500 liberaciones al año.

La credibilidad del ministerio no es solo su propia afirmación: CSI tiene una calificación de cuatro estrellas de Charity Navigator (2025) y el premio "Give with Confidence" de MinistryWatch. Para los lectores de Abba Post, la matemática es tan simple como confrontadora: el precio de la libertad de una persona es menos que la compra mensual de una familia. Oremos por los rescatadores, por los liberados y por las decenas de miles que aún esperan.

As the Tampa Bay Rays open the 2026 World Series against the Milwaukee Brewers this week, their ace pitcher Drew Rasmussen carries something onto the mound that no scouting report measures. "I would say 2017 was when it really hit me that everything the world promises to you is temporary. It's fleeting. There's no long-term joy," he said in a Travel Chapel video interview profiled by Sports Spectrum on October 22. "When you lean on the things of this earth, you're just going to find empty promises. They might bring you momentary happiness, but it's not everlasting joy."

The testimony was forged in injury. Drafted by the Rays in the first round of the 2017 MLB Draft out of Oregon State, Rasmussen failed his physical and never signed. An elbow injury in 2016 had already required Tommy John surgery; after returning in 2017 for eight games and four starts, he needed a second Tommy John that September and missed all of 2018. The Brewers took him in the sixth round in 2018; he debuted in the majors in August 2020 and was traded to the Rays in May 2021.

Rasmussen grew up going to church every week, but his faith matured at Oregon State after a Fellowship of Christian Athletes leader poured into him — and it was his college injuries that drove him deeper into Scripture. He has been married to his wife Stevie since 2020 and is now a two-time American League All-Star.

His road to the World Series ran straight through Cleveland: in ALCS Game 5 on October 18, Rasmussen threw seven dominant innings — one run, four hits, six strikeouts, one walk — in a 3–1 Rays win, and Tampa Bay closed out the series in Game 6 on October 19, 7–3.

When he takes the ball in the Fall Classic, Rasmussen will be pitching for more than a championship ring. He is living proof of his own sermon: the promises of this world expire, but the joy he found in Christ — in hospital rooms and rehab setbacks — never did.

Moving the roughly 25-ton bath, about 13 feet across, intact was an engineering feat: steel beams inserted beneath the structure, gauze and foam protecting the ancient plaster, and a 300-ton crane lifting it onto a wide-body truck for transport to the IAA's National Treasures Department in Jerusalem for study. "The Jewish settlement we discovered here is the first and the only known one in this area… in the seam between the Judean Lowlands and the northern Negev no Jewish settlement was known until now," said lead archaeologist Elena Kogan-Zahavi. Stone measuring vessels widely used by Jews of the period were also found, and the findings suggest the settlement was abandoned during the Bar Kokhba Revolt in the second century C.E.

For believers, the discovery is a tangible confirmation of the world Scripture describes: real Jewish communities, real roads, real baths in the world of Jesus' day. Two millennia later, the stones still testify — and the ground of Israel keeps reminding us that the Bible was never written in a fairy-tale land.

La Autoridad de Antigüedades de Israel anunció el descubrimiento y traslado de un mikvé notablemente conservado de 2.000 años de antigüedad —un baño ritual judío— en el barrio Carmei Gat de Kiryat Gat, en el sur de Israel. Es la primera evidencia de un asentamiento judío previamente desconocido que data del período del Segundo Templo: la era del ministerio de Jesús. Anunciado a finales de septiembre, el descubrimiento sigue en los medios esta semana.

Mover intacto el baño de unas 25 toneladas y unos 13 pies de ancho fue una hazaña de ingeniería: vigas de acero insertadas bajo la estructura, gasa y espuma protegiendo el yeso antiguo, y una grúa de 300 toneladas levantándolo sobre un camión de plataforma ancha para su transporte al Departamento de Tesoros Nacionales de la IAA en Jerusalén para su estudio. Será devuelto a Carmei Gat para exhibición pública una vez que terminen las obras del barrio.

«El asentamiento judío que descubrimos aquí es el primero y el único conocido en esta zona… en la franja entre las tierras bajas de Judea y el norte del Néguev no se conocía ningún asentamiento judío hasta ahora», dijo la arqueóloga líder Elena Kogan-Zahavi. También se encontraron recipientes de piedra para medir, ampliamente usados por los judíos de la época, y los hallazgos sugieren que el asentamiento fue abandonado durante la revuelta de Bar Kojba en el siglo II d. C.

Para los creyentes, el descubrimiento es una confirmación tangible del mundo que describen las Escrituras: comunidades judías reales, caminos reales, baños reales en el mundo de los días de Jesús. Dos milenios después, las piedras aún testifican, y el suelo de Israel sigue recordándonos que la Biblia nunca fue escrita en una tierra de cuentos.

Samaritan's Purse's Operation Christmas Child has kicked off its 2026 shoebox-packing season this October, with churches across the United States and Canada distributing shoeboxes and preparing for National Collection Week, November 16–23, 2026.

The numbers behind the ministry are staggering: more than 244 million gift-filled shoeboxes have been collected and delivered to children in more than 170 countries and territories since 1993. This year's goal is to reach 12.8 million children with the hope of Jesus — many of them suffering from war, poverty, disease and disaster. Nearly 5,000 drop-off locations will open across the United States in November, plus more than 300 in Canada, where Canadians packed 458,768 boxes last year. Participants can donate $10 per box for a "Follow Your Box" tracking label.

The project partners with local churches worldwide to share the Gospel alongside the gifts. This year's theme: "It's all about Jesus!" — from Matthew 19:14.

"Shoebox gifts may seem simple, but they have an eternal impact. Each box is an opportunity to tell a boy or girl in need about the true meaning of Christmas," Samaritan's Purse says. "I've delivered these boxes around the world, and I never get tired of seeing the joy that they bring — even in the midst of disasters, war, and poverty."

Behind every shoebox is a kitchen table, a church classroom, a child packing for another child they will never meet. Christmas is still weeks away — but somewhere in November, 12.8 million of those small boxes will open, and with each one, the story of Jesus.
