First Spanish church joins the new Global Anglican CommunionLa primera iglesia española se une a la nueva Comunión Anglicana Global

A small evangelical Anglican congregation in Valencia has become the first church in Spain to join the new Global Anglican Communion, a movement of churches seeking a return to historic Christian faith and the centrality of the Bible.Una pequeña congregación anglicana evangélica de Valencia se convirtió en la primera iglesia de España en unirse a la nueva Comunión Anglicana Global, un movimiento de iglesias que busca volver a la fe cristiana histórica y a la centralidad de la Biblia.

A first congregation in Spain has formally joined the confessional Anglican evangelical movement that is breaking away from Canterbury. The Comunidad Anglicana Evangélica de Valencia, a community of around thirty people led by pastor Julian Milson, is now a member of the Anglican Convocation Europe.

The congregation was established in 2025 after Milson, who is of English origin, left the historic Spanish Anglican Church, the IERE, along with almost the entire congregation he had served for six years in Valencia. The group now holds its Sunday services in a hotel and meets for Bible studies during the week in people's homes.

The move is part of a much larger realignment. In the Nigerian capital, Abuja, 347 bishops from 27 Anglican provinces across all continents formally launched the new Global Anglican Communion in March 2026, seeking a return to historic Christian faith and the centrality of Scripture. The movement no longer takes part in the Lambeth Conferences or the meetings of primates, and no longer recognises the spiritual authority of the Archbishop of Canterbury.

For the Valencia congregation, the decision was about conscience and clarity. Its leaders say the role of a pastor must be to call everyone to repentance and faith in Jesus Christ, so that many may come to know the good news of salvation.

The small church's step is a reminder that the great movements of church history are often carried forward by small, faithful congregations willing to pay a price for what they believe.
