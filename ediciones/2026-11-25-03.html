Life Surge Moves Into $16 Million Headquarters as Attendance Falls by HalfLife Surge estrena sede millonaria mientras su asistencia se desploma a la mitad

The Christian wealth seminar company has moved into a $16 million, 50,000-square-foot headquarters in Florida even as average event attendance has fallen from about 4,000 to under 2,000.La empresa de seminarios cristianos sobre riqueza se mudó a una sede de 16 millones de dólares y 50.000 pies cuadrados en Florida, mientras su asistencia promedio cayó de unos 4.000 a menos de 2.000 por evento.

November 23, 202623 de noviembre de 2026

Life Surge, the traveling Christian wealth seminar company, has moved into a new corporate headquarters at 908 Riverside Drive in Palmetto, Florida, a 50,000-square-foot building that cost $16 million. The move comes at a difficult moment for the company, which faces accusations of pressuring Christian attendees to invest tens of thousands of dollars in real estate and stock courses. Life Surge denies any wrongdoing.

According to reporting by Mother Jones journalist Kiera Butler on the podcast "Pablo Torre Finds Out," average attendance at Life Surge events has fallen from about 4,000 per event in the first five months of 2026 to fewer than 2,000 this fall. The podcast also reported layoffs of at least 85 employees — more than half the staff — since early November, and a cut from 32 to 36 events a year to a dozen or fewer in 2027.

Several prominent figures have distanced themselves from the company, including NFL quarterback Tim Tebow, Boston Celtics coach Joe Mazzulla, and pastor Craig Groeschel. In 2025, Life Surge's one-day conference drew more than 117,000 attendees across its tour, a scale that made it one of the most visible Christian events in the country.

The contrast between the new headquarters and the shrinking crowds raises a question many Christians are now asking: when does a ministry built on teaching stewardship start to look like a business built on selling it? Life Surge's defenders say the company teaches practical skills with a biblical foundation; its critics say the high-priced courses exploit the trust of believers.

For now, the company has not responded in detail to the latest reporting. But the episode is a cautionary tale for the church: a famous name on a poster is not a substitute for discernment, and no stage — however large, however well-lit — can replace the slow, ordinary work of teaching God's people to handle money with wisdom and contentment.

En 2025, la conferencia de un día de Life Surge atrajo a más de 117.000 asistentes en su gira, una escala que la convirtió en uno de los eventos cristianos más visibles del país.

El contraste entre la nueva sede y las multitudes que se achican plantea una pregunta que muchos cristianos se hacen ahora: ¿cuándo un ministerio construido para enseñar mayordomía empieza a parecer un negocio construido para venderla? Los defensores de Life Surge dicen que la empresa enseña habilidades prácticas con fundamento bíblico; sus críticos dicen que los cursos caros explotan la confianza de los creyentes.

Pero el episodio es una advertencia para la iglesia: un nombre famoso en un cartel no sustituye el discernimiento, y ningún escenario —por grande y bien iluminado que sea— puede reemplazar el trabajo lento y ordinario de enseñar al pueblo de Dios a manejar el dinero con sabiduría y contentamiento.
