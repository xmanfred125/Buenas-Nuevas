Comedian Druski — Drew Desbordes — has signed a multi-year deal with Netflix through his company, 4LIFERS Entertainment. The first project under the agreement, 'Collect & Praise', premieres on December 1: a semi-scripted comedy in which Druski plays a money-hungry megachurch pastor for whom, in the show's own framing, 'faith comes second to fortune', with Mike Epps as his unpredictable deacon. Netflix will also license his 'Coulda Been' catalog, which has passed one billion views on YouTube. The deal was announced on November 2.

The character at the center of the show is already famous. It went viral in January 2026 with the sketch 'Mega Church Pastors Love Money', and Druski performed it at the BET Awards in June. The comedy lands because it targets something real: the prosperity preaching that has made a handful of pastors wealthy while their congregations are told that giving is the measure of faith.

Druski's rise reached another milestone on November 7, when he hosted Saturday Night Live with The Strokes as musical guest — billed as the first digital content creator to host the show. For a comedian who built his audience on a phone screen, the SNL stage is the establishment's seal of approval, and the Netflix deal is the business end of the same ascent.

The show is satire, not endorsement, and Christians will watch it with mixed feelings. A comedy that mocks pastors who love money can be a useful mirror — the prophets, after all, had sharp words for shepherds who fed themselves instead of the flock. But satire that treats the pulpit itself as the joke risks something else: teaching an audience to laugh at the church rather than to long for a better one. 'Collect & Praise' will test which of those two things it is.

Pero la sátira que trata el púlpito mismo como el chiste corre otro riesgo: enseñar a la audiencia a reírse de la iglesia en lugar de anhelar una mejor. «Collect & Praise» pondrá a prueba cuál de las dos cosas es.
