The New York Jets beat the Los Angeles Chargers 27-24 on Sunday, November 22, at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, snapping a five-game losing streak. It was only the Jets' third win of the season — and the night belonged to a 33-year-old wide receiver who had spent most of the year waiting for a chance.

Sterling Shepard caught two passes for 24 yards and two touchdowns, both from Geno Smith: one of 8 yards in the second quarter and one of 16 yards in the fourth. It was the first two-touchdown-receiving game in his 11-year, 120-game NFL career. He had not scored more than one touchdown in a season since 2020, and his first touchdown on Sunday was his first in three years.

Shepard spent most of the season on the Jets' practice squad. Earlier in 2026 he was with the Houston Texans, who cut him after six days. He played his first eight seasons with the New York Giants, where he was once a starter — a long way from the practice squad, the phone that does not ring, and the season that looked finished.

After the game, Shepard did not talk about the playbook or the route running. "It's a true testament to the Lord and to being patient, bro," he said, describing his season as "a roller coaster." For a player who has spent a year on the edge of the roster, the words were not a cliché but a summary of how he survived it.

Geno Smith completed 17 of 21 passes for 176 yards and three touchdowns; the Jets entered the game at 2-8. The win changes little about the Jets' season. But for Shepard, the night was proof of something he had been holding onto all year: that patience is not the same as giving up, and that the Lord's timing is not the depth chart's.
