Sophie Cunningham Draws Christian Pushback Over Topless Maxim ShootSophie Cunningham divide a los cristianos tras su portada en topless en Maxim

Sophie Cunningham, the 30-year-old WNBA guard now a free agent after her season with the Indiana Fever, appears on the cover of Maxim magazine's November/December issue — and posed topless inside it, her hair covering her chest. The shoot has divided Christians who, until now, had celebrated her as one of the most outspoken believers in women's basketball.

Cunningham was baptized about seven months ago at Christ's Church of the Valley in Phoenix, and she has won wide admiration this year for opposing male athletes competing in women's sports. In the Maxim interview, she said she is looking for a Christ-centered husband who will lead her in her walk with Jesus.

Princeton professor Robert P. George wrote on Facebook that he can no longer recommend her as a role model: "Her pornifying herself in photographs for Maxim magazine makes that impossible." Evangelist Justin Peters criticized pastor Josh Howerton for presenting her on November 16 as "a GREAT example" of how to share the Gospel.

The disagreement is not really about one photo shoot. It is about what Christian witness looks like when the spotlight is brightest — whether a believer's platform belongs to the faith she professes, or whether faith is one more part of a personal brand to be displayed alongside everything else.

Cunningham's defenders note that she is a new believer, still growing, and that grace should cover a misstep. Her critics answer that a public platform carries public responsibility, and that the church does a young Christian no favor by applauding what it should gently correct. Both sides, in their way, are saying the same thing: that the world is watching, and that it notices when the life and the confession do not match.
