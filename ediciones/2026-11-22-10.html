Police Drop Case Against Watford Street Preacher Arrested for Criticizing IslamLa policía archiva el caso contra el predicador callejero de Watford detenido por criticar el islam

Hertfordshire Police have closed their investigation into Pastor Steve Maile, 66, senior pastor of Oasis City Church in Watford, and will take no further action — seven months after he was handcuffed on Watford High Street while preaching the Gospel, singing hymns and criticizing Islam.

Maile was arrested on April 18, 2026, initially on suspicion of assault — a claim he denied, which was quickly dropped — and a religiously and racially aggravated public order offence. The Crown Prosecution Service decided the evidence did not meet the threshold to charge him, and the case has now been closed without any charges ever being formally filed.

Supported by the Christian Legal Centre, Maile says he will return to street preaching and is considering a civil claim against the police. For him, the seven months of uncertainty were themselves a punishment — months in which a pastor's public ministry was shadowed by the threat of prosecution.

The case has become a touchstone in Britain's debate over free speech and street evangelism. Supporters of Maile argue that preaching the Gospel in public, including criticism of other faiths, is a right protected by law — and that the arrest itself, whatever its outcome, sends a chilling message to other street preachers.

Maile's vindication will be welcomed by Christians across Britain who have watched similar cases with growing concern. The question now is whether the police will learn from it — or whether another preacher, on another high street, will face the same ordeal.
