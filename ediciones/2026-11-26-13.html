Angel Studios' newest film arrives in U.S. theaters on Wednesday, November 25, Thanksgiving eve. “Hershey” is a biographical drama about Milton S. Hershey, the founder of The Hershey Company, and the unlikely road from repeated business failure to one of the most famous names in American chocolate.

The film stars Finn Wittrock as Milton Hershey and Alexandra Daddario as his wife Catherine “Kitty” Hershey, with Alan Ruck, Richard Kind, David Costabile and Heléne Yorke in supporting roles. It is directed by Mark Waters, known for “Mean Girls” and “Freaky Friday,” and written by Sharon Paul and Timothy Michael Hayes. It is a Dandelion Media production made in partnership with the Hershey Entities, including The Hershey Company, the Milton Hershey School, Hershey Entertainment & Resorts, the M.S. Hershey Foundation and the Hershey Trust Company.

The story follows Hershey's string of failed businesses before he built his chocolate empire, and how he and Kitty channeled that success into something that outlasted them both: a legacy of education and community in the form of the Milton Hershey School, which continues today. The film is not overtly preachy, though the Mennonite faith background of Hershey's mother is present in the story.

The film premiered on Tuesday, November 17, at the Historic Hershey Theatre in Hershey, Pennsylvania, and on Thursday, November 19, Wittrock, Daddario and Waters lit the Empire State Building in New York City to celebrate the release. It is rated PG for thematic elements — a family death and abandonment — and one instance of language; Angel Studios suggests it for ages 8 and up.

Early reviews from secular critics have been mixed to negative, but the film is positioned as a wholesome, family-friendly Thanksgiving release, arriving alongside other Angel Studios faith titles in theaters, including “Drummer Boy” and “Jimmy.” For families looking for a holiday film about perseverance and giving back, “Hershey” offers a true American story: a man who failed again and again, built something lasting, and gave most of it away to educate children.

Para las familias que buscan una película navideña sobre perseverancia y generosidad, “Hershey” ofrece una verdadera historia estadounidense: un hombre que fracasó una y otra vez, construyó algo duradero y entregó la mayor parte para educar a los niños.
