Bible Apologist Wes Huff Apologizes for Plagiarism and Steps Aside from MinistryEl apologista bíblico Wes Huff pide perdón por plagio y se aparta del ministerio

Wes Huff, the Canadian apologist whose videos defending the Bible have been viewed nearly 30 million times, admitted he plagiarized academic work and said he will step aside from Apologetics Canada and church ministry.Wes Huff, el apologista canadiense cuyos videos en defensa de la Biblia suman casi 30 millones de vistas, admitió haber plagiado trabajos académicos y anunció que se apartará de Apologetics Canada y del ministerio en la iglesia.

November 19, 202619 de noviembre de 2026

Wesley "Wes" Huff, a Canadian Christian apologist whose videos defending the historical accuracy of the Bible have been viewed nearly 30 million times, apologized on Thursday, November 19, for plagiarizing his academic work. In a post on X, he wrote plainly: "I have plagiarized the work of others. This was wrong, and it was sinful. Over the years, I allowed myself to develop justifications for my actions, minimizing their seriousness."

The plagiarism took place during his studies at Tyndale Seminary, where he received a master's degree, and at the University of Toronto, where he was a doctoral student. Huff said he would step aside from Apologetics Canada, where he served as a vice president and speaker, and from church ministry, submitting himself to the care of his church and of Apologetics Canada's leadership.

Huff first became widely known after appearing on the Joe Rogan podcast in January 2025, an episode viewed nearly 10 million times on YouTube. The appearance helped build his audience tenfold, and his own YouTube channel now has more than a million subscribers, making him one of the most visible young voices in Christian apologetics.

The allegations surfaced in a video posted on November 8 by scholar and social media influencer Dan McClellan, who said he found that much of one of Huff's papers included the work of a different scholar, and then found more instances in other papers. Huff initially responded in a video saying the work was mediocre writing, not plagiarism; that video was later deleted.

Apologetics Canada said it had been aware of the allegations for weeks and is providing counseling and support to Huff and his family — his young daughter was hospitalized around the time the allegations became public. The ministry said it will conduct a full review with independent third parties over the coming months, and a video course Huff taught on the historical reliability of the Bible has been removed from Peterson Academy, the online site started by Jordan Peterson.

McClellan suggested the seminary where Huff studied might need to rescind his master's degree, saying plagiarism "calls into question Huff's academic integrity." For a ministry built on defending the trustworthiness of Scripture, the episode is a painful reminder that credibility is not defended by arguments alone, but by the life of the one making them.

Las acusaciones salieron a la luz en un video publicado el 8 de noviembre por el académico e influencer Dan McClellan, quien afirmó que gran parte de uno de los trabajos de Huff incluía el trabajo de otro académico, y luego encontró más casos en otros trabajos. Huff respondió al principio con un video en el que decía que se trataba de escritura mediocre, no de plagio; ese video fue eliminado después.

El ministerio anunció que realizará una revisión completa con terceros independientes en los próximos meses, y un curso en video de Huff sobre la confiabilidad histórica de la Biblia fue retirado de Peterson Academy, el sitio en línea creado por Jordan Peterson.

McClellan sugirió que el seminario donde estudió Huff podría necesitar revocarle la maestría, al afirmar que el plagio «pone en duda la integridad académica de Huff». Para un ministerio construido sobre la defensa de la confiabilidad de las Escrituras, el episodio es un recordatorio doloroso de que la credibilidad no se defiende solo con argumentos, sino con la vida de quien los presenta.
