Cody Carnes and Kari Jobe Give the 350-Year-Old Doxology Six New VersesCody Carnes y Kari Jobe dan seis estrofas nuevas a la Doxología, de 350 años

The husband-and-wife worship leaders release 'Doxology (All Praise)' — their first new song together in several years, expanding Thomas Ken's 1674 hymn.Los esposos y líderes de adoración lanzan «Doxology (All Praise)», su primera canción nueva juntos en varios años, ampliando el himno de Thomas Ken de 1674.

Worship leaders and songwriters Cody Carnes and Kari Jobe Carnes have released a new song together, 'Doxology (All Praise)', available from today. It is their first new song released together in several years, and it brings a fresh expression of worship to one of the most recognisable hymns in Christian history — expanding the timeless words of the Doxology with six newly written verses.

The Doxology was originally written in 1674 by Thomas Ken, and has been sung by generations of believers worldwide. Its four famous lines — 'Praise God, from whom all blessings flow' — remain one of the most widely recognised expressions of praise in Christian worship. The Carnes' new version keeps the hymn's heart and stretches it: six new verses that give congregations fresh language for the same ancient praise.

The release is a family affair in more ways than one. Cody and Kari Jobe Carnes are among the most recognised collaborations in modern worship music; their joint recording of 'The Blessing' has resonated with congregations around the world, and most recently the duo won a 2026 GMA Dove Award for Inspirational Recorded Song of the Year for their rendition of 'The Blessing' alongside tenor Andrea Bocelli.

The single's artwork carries its own story: it features 'Agnus Dei, Cordero Místico', a painting from the late 18th century by the renowned Puerto Rican painter José Campeche y Jordán — a reminder that the song's roots run through the whole history of the Church, in every language and every century.

For worship leaders looking for something both old and new this Sunday, 'Doxology (All Praise)' offers a rare combination: words the congregation already knows by heart, and words they have never sung before. Three and a half centuries after Thomas Ken first wrote them, the blessings are still flowing — and the Church is still finding new ways to say so.

Tres siglos y medio después de que Thomas Ken las escribiera por primera vez, las bendiciones siguen fluyendo, y la Iglesia sigue encontrando nuevas formas de decirlo.
