Episcopal Church Has Nearly 350,000 Fewer Members Than a Decade AgoLa Iglesia Episcopal tiene casi 350.000 miembros menos que hace una década

The Episcopal Church's 2025 Parochial Report analysis shows about 1.425 million members — nearly 350,000 fewer than a decade ago — with Sunday attendance falling to 407,000.El análisis del Informe Parroquial 2025 de la Iglesia Episcopal registra unos 1,425 millones de miembros —casi 350.000 menos que hace una década— y una asistencia dominical reducida a 407.000.

Abba Post · October 18, 2026Abba Post · 18 de octubre de 2026

The Episcopal Church has released an analysis of its 2025 Parochial Report, confirming that the denomination's long-running decline continued last year. According to the report, the liberal Mainline church counted about 1.425 million members in 2025 — nearly 350,000 fewer than the roughly 1.77 million members it reported in 2015, and down from about 1.547 million in 2023.

"Membership continues its slow decline, a trend that has persisted for decades," the report states. The erosion extends to every measure of church life: in-person Sunday worship attendance fell from about 600,000 in 2015 to roughly 407,000 last year, slightly below the 413,000 reported for 2024. The number of open parishes and missions slipped from 6,707 to 6,673, and baptisms dipped from 19,624 in 2024 to 19,316 in 2025.

The numbers complete a trajectory that has alarmed the denomination for years. In 2010, the Episcopal Church's number of active baptized members dropped below 2 million; a decade and a half later, it has lost roughly another half million. Many observers connect the exodus to the denomination's increasingly progressive theological direction — after the consecration of Gene Robinson as its first openly gay bishop in 2003, scores of congregations and two dioceses left in protest, and the departure of traditionalist churches has continued.

The denomination did not publish a total membership count for 2024, saying at the time that a revision of the questions used to measure membership had created confusion in how churches reported the figure. The 2025 analysis restores a clear count — and with it, a sobering verdict: the slow bleeding has not stopped.

For American evangelicals watching from the sidelines, the Episcopal story stands as a cautionary tale about what happens when a church trades the authority of Scripture for cultural accommodation. May it also be a call to intercession: behind every statistic is a parish, a family, and a soul. Let us pray that those who have drifted would find their way back to the unchanging Gospel, and that faithful congregations remaining in the denomination would hold fast without compromise.

«La membresía continúa su lenta disminución, una tendencia que ha persistido por décadas», declara el informe. La erosión alcanza todos los indicadores de la vida eclesial: la asistencia dominical presencial cayó de unos 600.000 en 2015 a cerca de 407.000 el año pasado, ligeramente por debajo de los 413.000 reportados en 2024. El número de parroquias y misiones abiertas bajó de 6.707 a 6.673, y los bautismos descendieron de 19.624 en 2024 a 19.316 en 2025.

Las cifras completan una trayectoria que preocupa a la denominación desde hace años. En 2010, el número de miembros bautizados activos cayó por debajo de los 2 millones; una década y media después, se ha perdido otro medio millón. Muchos observadores vinculan el éxodo con la orientación teológica cada vez más progresista de la iglesia: tras la consagración de Gene Robinson como primer obispo abiertamente homosexual en 2003, decenas de congregaciones y dos diócesis se marcharon en protesta, y la salida de iglesias tradicionalistas ha continuado.

India's Proposed Law Could Let the State Seize Church Assets, Advocates WarnUna ley propuesta en India podría permitir al Estado confiscar bienes de la Iglesia, advierten defensores

Advocates urge Secretary of State Marco Rubio to press India over proposed FCRA amendments that would let the government seize the assets of Christian charities and churches on vague grounds of harming "religious harmony."Defensores instan al secretario de Estado Marco Rubio a presionar a India por las enmiendas a la FCRA que permitirían al gobierno confiscar los bienes de organizaciones e iglesias cristianas bajo el vago argumento de dañar la «armonía religiosa».

Abba Post · October 17, 2026Abba Post · 17 de octubre de 2026

With U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio on a diplomatic trip to India this week, advocates for religious freedom are urging Washington to press New Delhi over proposed amendments to the Foreign Contribution Regulation Act (FCRA) that could devastate Christian charities — and even allow the state to seize churches, hospitals and schools.

The FCRA already requires civil society organizations to hold a license to receive foreign funding, and more than 20,000 licenses have been cancelled since 2010 — including those of prominent Christian bodies such as the Evangelical Fellowship of India, and even Mother Teresa's Missionaries of Charity, whose license was revoked and then reinstated after global outcry.

The proposed amendments would go further still. Under the new language, a charity's license could be revoked merely if the government believes its work negatively affects "social or religious harmony" — an undefined phrase that, in a country where Christians regularly face mob violence and laws punishing conversion, leaves every faith-based organization exposed. Once a license is lost, the amendments would let the Indian government take control of the organization's assets and use them as it sees fit, with nearly no due process to stop it.

Recently approved regulations also expressly prohibit the use of foreign funds for "proselytizing." As advocate Andrea Picciotti-Bayer warns, the missionary legacy that built India's Christian hospitals, orphanages and schools — the work St. Francis Xavier began in Goa in 1542 — can no longer be sustained if the very mention of the name of Jesus can be treated as a legal violation.

The stakes go beyond India's borders: foreign donations — even from American churches and agencies — sent to support legitimate charitable work could end up in the hands of the Indian state. Earlier this year, after Congressman Riley Moore publicly opposed the measure, the amendments were paused and sent to a reviewing committee. But they could return at any moment. The Catholic Association of Goa has called for the bill's withdrawal.

This is a moment for the global church to stand with its Indian brothers and sisters. Let us pray that Secretary Rubio raises these concerns firmly, that India's leaders choose religious freedom over state control, and that the centuries-old legacy of Christian mission and charity in India continues to thrive for generations to come.

Con el secretario de Estado estadounidense Marco Rubio de gira diplomática en India esta semana, los defensores de la libertad religiosa instan a Washington a presionar a Nueva Delhi por las enmiendas propuestas a la Ley de Regulación de Contribuciones Extranjeras (FCRA), que podrían devastar a las organizaciones cristianas de beneficencia e incluso permitir al Estado expropiar iglesias, hospitales y escuelas.

La FCRA ya exige que las organizaciones de la sociedad civil obtengan una licencia para recibir fondos del extranjero, y más de 20.000 licencias han sido canceladas desde 2010, incluidas las de organizaciones cristianas prominentes como la Evangelical Fellowship of India, e incluso las Misioneras de la Caridad de la Madre Teresa, cuya licencia fue revocada y luego reinstituida tras el clamor mundial.

Las enmiendas propuestas irían aún más lejos. Con la nueva redacción, la licencia de una organización podría revocarse si el gobierno considera que su labor afecta negativamente la «armonía social o religiosa», una frase indefinida que, en un país donde los cristianos sufren regularmente la violencia de turbas y leyes que castigan la conversión, deja expuesta a toda organización de fe. Una vez perdida la licencia, las enmiendas permitirían al gobierno indio tomar el control de los activos de la organización y usarlos como le parezca, casi sin debido proceso para impedirlo.

Las regulaciones recientemente aprobadas también prohíben expresamente el uso de fondos extranjeros para «proselitismo». Como advierte la defensora Andrea Picciotti-Bayer, el legado misionero que construyó los hospitales, orfanatos y escuelas cristianas de India —la obra que San Francisco Javier inició en Goa en 1542— ya no podrá sostenerse si la sola mención del nombre de Jesús puede tratarse como una violación legal.

Lo que está en juego trasciende las fronteras de India: las donaciones extranjeras —incluso de iglesias y agencias estadounidenses— enviadas para apoyar obra benéfica legítima podrían terminar en manos del Estado indio. A principios de este año, tras la oposición pública del congresista Riley Moore, las enmiendas fueron pausadas y enviadas a un comité revisor. Pero podrían volver en cualquier momento. La Asociación Católica de Goa ha pedido el retiro del proyecto.

Este es un momento para que la iglesia global apoye a sus hermanos y hermanas de India. Oremos para que el secretario Rubio plantee estas preocupaciones con firmeza, para que los líderes de India elijan la libertad religiosa por encima del control estatal, y para que el legado centenario de la misión y la caridad cristiana en India siga prosperando por generaciones.

Terrorists believed to be Boko Haram overran the Christian farming town of Dzangola Kilba in Gombi County, Adamawa State, during the night of Thursday, October 15, killing 30 civilians, according to local reports. Eyewitnesses said an evangelist of the Church of the Brethren in Nigeria (EYN), identified as Barnabas David, was locked inside his house and burned alive.

The assault on the remote hilltop settlement in Guyaku District began around 9:00 p.m. Thursday and lasted through the night. Attackers burned eight houses and seven shops, and set fire to the local congregation of the EYN — the Ekklesiyar Yan'uwa a Nigeria, one of the country's largest indigenous Christian denominations — damaging part of the church building before the flames were stopped. Witnesses said there was no security intervention at any point during the raid.

This is the fourth time in six months that the same Christian farming belt across Hong and Gombi counties has been struck. On September 6, the same village was attacked in a raid that left 12 residents dead, destroyed a church and looted shops. In April, the Islamic State West Africa Province opened fire on a football pitch in nearby Guyaka, killing 33 bystanders.

Residents from Gunyaku, Tela-Bala, Sabon-Gari and Nzangula — more than 4,000 people — are sleeping on mats in Yola and Gombi town, while the farms they abandoned sit ripe for harvest. Abba Post · October 7, 2026Abba Post · 7 de octubre de 2026

Pakistan's Supreme Court has upheld the death sentence of Qaisar Ayub, a 49-year-old Christian father of three, under the country's blasphemy laws — while in the same October 7 judgment acquitting his brother Amoon on the same evidence. The split ruling has sent shockwaves through the global religious-freedom community.

The case dates to 2011, when allegations arose over a website registered in Qaisar's name. He has said he stopped publishing in 2009 and that another individual continued running the account under his name. Qaisar was arrested in 2014 and Amoon in 2015; in 2018 both brothers were sentenced to death, and the Lahore High Court upheld those convictions in 2022. After more than a decade behind bars, the brothers' final appeal produced opposite outcomes: Amoon walked free for insufficient evidence, while Qaisar remains in Sahiwal Jail facing execution.

"This is a deeply troubling moment for religious freedom in Pakistan and a ruling that should concern the entire international community," said Jordan Sekulow, executive director of the American Center for Law and Justice, whose Pakistani affiliate represented the brothers. "While we celebrate Amoon's acquittal after more than a decade behind bars, his brother Qaisar remains sentenced to death." More than 300,000 people have signed a petition calling for Qaisar's release.

The organization CLAAS, which supported the defense from the beginning, called the ruling devastating and warned about the inconsistency of the court's approach. Its UK director, Nasir Saeed, said the case illustrates a deeper pattern: "Because of threats from hardliners, lower courts pass their responsibility to the higher court, and then it takes years to prove the accused innocent." Pakistan's blasphemy laws — section 295-C of the penal code — mandate death for defiling the name of Muhammad, and false or opportunistic accusations have long been a source of terror for Christians and other minorities.

Qaisar Ayub's three children now live with the confirmed death sentence hanging over their father. Let us pray for Qaisar's life, for his family's endurance, and for the lawyers still fighting for him — and for a day when no one in Pakistan must fear the executioner's rope for the crime of being Christian.

La sentencia dividida ha conmocionado a la comunidad mundial de la libertad religiosa.

La excavación también sacó a la luz parte del pilar dorsal de la estatua con el nombre de Horus del rey, una segunda estatua real de casi dos metros y medio cuyo rostro se conserva intacto bajo el tocado nemes a rayas, y un bloque de granito rosa de más de un metro con inscripciones de los títulos reales «Sa-Ra» (Hijo de Ra) y «Nesw-Bity» (Rey del Alto y Bajo Egipto).

Ramsés II, nacido alrededor del 1303 a.C., fue uno de los gobernantes más poderosos del Imperio Nuevo egipcio, y la tradición cristiana lo ha asociado por siglos con el faraón sin nombre del libro del Éxodo que esclavizó a los israelitas y desafió a Moisés. La Escritura nunca nombra al faraón, y los eruditos siguen debatiendo la identificación. Pero para los creyentes, la imponente escala del monumento trae su propio sermón silencioso: el rey más poderoso de su época yace enterrado en fragmentos bajo el campo de un agricultor, mientras que el Dios que libró a Israel de su casa de servidumbre sigue siendo adorado hoy.

El ministro de Turismo y Antigüedades de Egipto, Sherif Fathy, viajó a la aldea para honrar personalmente al agricultor, entregándole a al-Shahat y a su esposa certificados de reconocimiento y un viaje de peregrinación Umrah. «El ministro insistió en que el ciudadano fuera honrado en su aldea, en su propia tierra y entre su familia y sus vecinos, en reconocimiento a su honestidad, conciencia y sentido de responsabilidad», dijo el ministerio. Las excavaciones continúan en el terreno mientras los investigadores buscan entender cómo encajan los monumentos en el sitio circundante, una zona conocida por un antiguo templo dedicado a Isis.

Christian Relief Groups Rush to Gulf Coast After Hurricane IsaiasGrupos cristianos de socorro se movilizan en la costa del Golfo tras el huracán Isaias

Days after Hurricane Isaias battered the Florida Panhandle and southern Alabama, Samaritan's Purse, Operation Blessing and other Christian ministries are on the ground clearing debris, serving meals and sharing the hope of the Gospel.Días después de que el huracán Isaias azotara el Panhandle de Florida y el sur de Alabama, Samaritan's Purse, Operation Blessing y otros ministerios cristianos están en el terreno retirando escombros, sirviendo comidas y compartiendo la esperanza del Evangelio.

Abba Post · October 15, 2026Abba Post · 15 de octubre de 2026

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, on the night of October 9 as a Category 2 storm with sustained winds of about 105 miles per hour, killing at least four people and knocking out power to hundreds of thousands of customers across the Florida Panhandle and southern Alabama. Within days, Christian disaster-relief organizations were on the ground, transforming local churches into bases of operation and bringing both practical help and spiritual encouragement to storm-weary families.

Samaritan's Purse deployed Disaster Relief Units — tractor-trailers filled with tools and equipment — to Florida and Alabama, establishing bases at Immanuel Baptist Church in Pace, Florida, and First Baptist Church in Brewton, Alabama. Volunteer teams began work on October 12, cutting felled trees, clearing debris and tarping damaged roofs, while Billy Graham Rapid Response Team chaplains serve alongside them, offering prayer and a listening ear. The ministry had received nearly 800 work orders from homeowners by midweek. "Two of our Disaster Relief Units — a tractor trailer filled with tools and equipment — are in Florida and Alabama. Volunteers are helping to cut felled trees, clear out debris, and more, and share the hope of the Gospel with families reeling from the storm," Samaritan's Purse said.

Operation Blessing centered its response in Pensacola, operating out of Brownsville Church after pre-positioning its disaster team in neighboring Alabama ahead of the storm. The charity has distributed at least 684 disaster relief kits provided by the Home Depot Foundation, 5,760 hygiene kits, more than 700 hot meals, 240 meal kits and 23 pallets of bottled water. "Pensacola took a pretty good beating," Operation Blessing President Drew Friedrich said, noting that over 300,000 people lost power in the area and about 175,000 were still without power as of Monday afternoon.

They are not alone. Southern Baptist Disaster Relief volunteers were serving meals in Pensacola, Convoy of Hope deployed to the Gulf Coast with relief kits, shelf-stable food and bottled water, and Eight Days of Hope delivered 1,800 hot meals from Destiny Worship Center. Mercy Chefs partnered with Jubilee Church to serve freshly prepared meals. "We want to come with the hope of the Gospel, let them know that they're seen, let them know that God sees them and He has not forgotten them and that we're here to help them," said a Samaritan's Purse spokeswoman. In the rubble left by Isaias, the church has once again shown up with chainsaws, hot meals and the good news.

El huracán Isaias tocó tierra cerca de Destin, Florida, la noche del 9 de octubre como tormenta de categoría 2 con vientos sostenidos de unos 168 kilómetros por hora, causando la muerte de al menos cuatro personas y dejando sin electricidad a cientos de miles de hogares en el Panhandle de Florida y el sur de Alabama. En cuestión de días, organizaciones cristianas de socorro ante desastres llegaron al terreno, convirtiendo iglesias locales en bases de operaciones y llevando ayuda práctica y aliento espiritual a familias agotadas por la tormenta.

Samaritan's Purse desplegó Unidades de Socorro ante Desastres —camiones con remolque llenos de herramientas y equipos— a Florida y Alabama, estableciendo bases en la Iglesia Bautista Immanuel en Pace, Florida, y la Primera Iglesia Bautista en Brewton, Alabama. Equipos de voluntarios comenzaron a trabajar el 12 de octubre, cortando árboles caídos, retirando escombros y cubriendo techos dañados con lonas, mientras capellanes del Equipo de Respuesta Rápida de Billy Graham sirven junto a ellos, ofreciendo oración y un oído atento. El ministerio había recibido casi 800 solicitudes de ayuda de propietarios para mediados de semana. «Dos de nuestras Unidades de Socorro ante Desastres —un camión con remolque lleno de herramientas y equipos— están en Florida y Alabama. Los voluntarios están ayudando a cortar árboles caídos, retirar escombros y más, y compartiendo la esperanza del Evangelio con familias golpeadas por la tormenta», dijo Samaritan's Purse.

Operation Blessing centró su respuesta en Pensacola, operando desde la iglesia Brownsville después de preposicionar su equipo de desastres en la vecina Alabama antes de la tormenta. La organización ha distribuido al menos 684 kits de socorro proporcionados por la Fundación Home Depot, 5.760 kits de higiene, más de 700 comidas calientes, 240 kits de comida y 23 tarimas de agua embotellada. «Pensacola recibió un buen golpe», dijo Drew Friedrich, presidente de Operation Blessing, señalando que más de 300.000 personas perdieron la electricidad en la zona y unas 175.000 seguían sin luz el lunes por la tarde.

No están solos. Voluntarios de Southern Baptist Disaster Relief sirvieron comidas en Pensacola, Convoy of Hope se desplegó en la costa del Golfo con kits de socorro, alimentos no perecederos y agua embotellada, y Eight Days of Hope entregó 1.800 comidas calientes desde el Destiny Worship Center. Mercy Chefs se asoció con la iglesia Jubilee para servir comidas recién preparadas. «Queremos llegar con la esperanza del Evangelio, que sepan que los vemos, que sepan que Dios los ve y no los ha olvidado, y que estamos aquí para ayudarlos», dijo una portavoz de Samaritan's Purse. Entre los escombros que dejó Isaias, la iglesia una vez más se hizo presente con motosierras, comidas calientes y las buenas nuevas.
