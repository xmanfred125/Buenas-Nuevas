Persecution Worsened in 55% of Countries of Concern, New Report FindsLa persecución empeoró en el 55% de los países bajo vigilancia, según un nuevo informe

A new report covering August 2024 to June 2026 finds that conditions for Christians worsened in 10 of 18 countries of concern and improved in none.Un nuevo informe que abarca de agosto de 2024 a junio de 2026 concluye que las condiciones para los cristianos empeoraron en 10 de 18 países bajo vigilancia y no mejoraron en ninguno.

Abba Post · October 27, 2026Abba Post · 27 de octubre de 2026

London, England, woke on Tuesday to a sobering global picture. A new report on Christians oppressed for their faith finds that conditions worsened in 10 of the 18 countries it monitors closely — 55 percent — and improved in none of them.

The report, “Persecuted and Forgotten? A Report on Christians Oppressed for Their Faith 2024-26,” was published Tuesday by Aid to the Church in Need and covers August 2024 to June 2026. It assesses roughly 413 million Christians living in countries where religious freedom violations are of particular concern.

The Middle East draws some of the report's gravest warnings. Upheaval there has raised questions about Christianity's survival in its ancient heartland, with Syria's civil war, Islamist violence in the Levant and the aftermath of the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack all cited as pressures. Israeli operations in Gaza, the report says, killed or displaced thousands, with churches serving as sanctuaries for Christians fleeing the violence.

Elsewhere, the drivers differ but the direction is the same. The report points to authoritarian crackdowns in China, Iran and Nicaragua, “anti-conversion” laws in India, radical Islamist influence in Pakistan, and a war in Sudan that has damaged or destroyed more than 100 churches. In Nigeria, at least one million Christians are counted among more than 3.5 million internally displaced people.

The findings echo the broader picture drawn by Open Doors' World Watch List 2026, which estimates that about 388 million Christians face high, very high or extreme persecution or discrimination. For the churches in the 18 countries studied, the report's message is blunt: in two years of monitoring, not one country's situation got better.
