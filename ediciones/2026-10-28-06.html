Illinois School District Pays Over $100,000 After Restricting Christian After-School ClubUn distrito escolar de Illinois pagará más de $100.000 por restringir un club cristiano extracurricular

The Moline-Coal Valley district will pay $105,351.25 and grant Good News Clubs the same facility access as secular after-school groups, settling a Liberty Counsel lawsuit.El distrito de Moline-Coal Valley pagará 105.351,25 dólares y otorgará a los Good News Clubs el mismo acceso a las instalaciones que a los grupos extracurriculares seculares, al resolver una demanda de Liberty Counsel.

An Illinois school district will pay $105,351.25 and change its policies after restricting a Christian after-school program, settling a lawsuit that accused the district of treating the club worse than secular groups.

The Moline-Coal Valley School District agreed to the payment in attorney fees, costs and damages, and to give Good News Clubs the same facility access as other nonreligious after-school clubs. Good News Clubs are the after-school program of Child Evangelism Fellowship, and the suit was brought by Liberty Counsel.

The fellowship had free access to district facilities from 2017 to 2020. After 2020, the district reclassified the clubs as “Category II” church-affiliated groups, charged rental fees, and barred flyers and participation in “Backpack Nights” — while groups like the Boy Scouts remained Category I with free access.

Liberty Counsel said the district's superintendent, Dr. Rachel Savage, would not accept legal materials on the fellowship's rights or waive the fees, despite board policy allowing it. The superintendent and the Board of Education were named as defendants.

Liberty Counsel founder and chairman Mat Staver called the outcome a victory for free speech and religious freedom. The First Amendment, he said, “prohibits public schools from discriminating against Christian viewpoints regarding use of school facilities,” calling the settlement “a great victory for Child Evangelism Fellowship, parents, and students.”

La organización tuvo acceso gratuito a las instalaciones del distrito de 2017 a 2020. Después de 2020, el distrito reclasificó a los clubes como grupos afiliados a iglesias de «Categoría II», cobró tarifas de alquiler y prohibió los volantes y la participación en las «Noches de Mochila», mientras grupos como los Boy Scouts permanecieron en la Categoría I con acceso gratuito.

La superintendente y la Junta de Educación fueron señaladas como demandadas.

El fundador y presidente de Liberty Counsel, Mat Staver, calificó el resultado como una victoria para la libertad de expresión y la libertad religiosa.
