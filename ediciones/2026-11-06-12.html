'God With Us': an incarcerated teen's question — 'Can God forgive me?' — inspired a modern Nativity film'God With Us': la pregunta de un adolescente encarcelado —'¿Puede Dios perdonarme?'— inspiró una Natividad moderna

Filmmaker Jimmy Jenkins retells the Nativity in a contemporary African American community; the film opens in select theaters November 13 after a premiere that drew more than 4,000 people.El cineasta Jimmy Jenkins recrea la Natividad en una comunidad afroamericana contemporánea; la película se estrena en cines selectos el 13 de noviembre, tras un estreno que reunió a más de 4.000 personas.

Abba Post · November 3, 2026Abba Post · 3 de noviembre de 2026

Filmmaker Jimmy Jenkins visited a juvenile detention facility to share the Gospel and met a 16-year-old boy who believed his crimes — including murder — placed him beyond God's forgiveness. That encounter inspired 'God With Us,' a contemporary retelling of the Nativity set within an African American community.

The film follows Mary Anne Thomas (Ashley Olivia Fisher) and Joseph 'Junie' Love (Aspen Kennedy), childhood sweethearts facing a miraculous pregnancy. It was written and directed by Jenkins, his second feature after 'Sinners Wanted'; his father, Pastor John K. Jenkins Sr. of First Baptist Church of Glenarden in Maryland, is executive producer. The film is distributed by Blue Harbor Entertainment, with theatrical release by Variance Films, and opens in select theaters on November 13, 2026.

A premiere celebration drew more than 4,000 people at First Baptist Church of Glenarden in Upper Marlboro, Maryland, on October 24. The film opens with a disclaimer that it is not intended to rewrite the Gospel — Jenkins said he anticipated criticism of the unconventional approach.

A review published November 5 by Movieguide called the film a modern-day parable with a strong Christian, biblical worldview, frequent Scripture quotations and a message that God is always with us, while cautioning parents about violence, drug material and sexual-abuse references.
