Evangelical Pastor Expelled From His Village in Mexico After Refusing a Ceremony Against His FaithPastor evangélico expulsado de su pueblo en México por negarse a una ceremonia contraria a su fe

An evangelical pastor in Oaxaca was detained for five days and then expelled from his village after refusing to take part in a ceremony contrary to his faith, in the latest case of religious intolerance against evangelicals in Mexico.Un pastor evangélico de Oaxaca fue detenido cinco días y luego expulsado de su pueblo por negarse a participar en una ceremonia contraria a su fe, en el caso más reciente de intolerancia religiosa contra los evangélicos en México.

November 19, 202619 de noviembre de 2026

An evangelical pastor and his family have been forcibly displaced from their community in Oaxaca, Mexico, after he refused to take part in a religious ceremony contrary to his beliefs. Pastor Mariano Velásquez Martínez, who leads the Camino Nuevo y Vivo Church — a congregation of around 25 people — was detained for five days without charge by community authorities before his expulsion was announced publicly.

The case unfolded in Santiago Malacatepec, in the municipality of San Juan Mazatlán. In November 2025, community authorities appointed Velásquez Martínez as steward, a traditional role that involves organizing and financing festivities for the majority religion. The pastor accepted under an explicit agreement that his role would be limited to providing candles and flowers. But on January 15, 2026, another steward ordered him to light candles, kneel and pray before the image of St. James the Apostle.

When the pastor refused, the authorities ignored the agreed terms and detained him for five days. He was then tied up with a rope and brought before an assembly of around 180 men, where his expulsion from the community was announced. It was not the first such case in the village: the previous pastor of the same small congregation had also been displaced for religious reasons in 2023.

The expulsion is one of several recent cases of repression against evangelicals across Mexico. In the state of Chiapas, eleven evangelicals were arrested and imprisoned in Pinar Salinas, in the municipality of Zinacantán, after community authorities broke into a private home where they were holding a family worship service. They had refused to cooperate in a festival organized by traditionalist Catholics.

The cases come amid a tightening of the legal framework against forced internal displacement in Oaxaca. In September 2025, the State Congress passed a law to prevent, address and repair forced internal displacement, with prison sentences of between 10 and 18 years. Porfirio Flores Zúñiga, a lawyer and representative of the Fraternity of Pastors, has publicly urged the state Attorney General's office and the Ministry of the Interior to apply that legislation to Pastor Velásquez Martínez's case — and to make clear that in Mexico, no one should lose their home for refusing to worship against their conscience.
