Six United States lawmakers have written to Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi demanding the immediate release of Said Abdelrazek, a Christian convert from Islam who faces up to 25 years in prison or execution. The letter was sent on November 19, and a copy was also sent to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

The signatories are Senator Josh Hawley of Missouri and Representatives Riley Moore of West Virginia, Barry Moore of Alabama, Elijah Crane of Arizona, Earl “Buddy” Carter of Georgia and Marlin Stutzman of Indiana. Their intervention raises a case that has also been taken up by the British Parliament, the US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) and the US State Department.

Abdelrazek, also rendered Said Mansour Rezk Abdelrazek, was arrested in July 2025 on blasphemy and counter-terrorism charges. According to the letter, his “crimes” were peaceful online expression about his Christian faith and efforts to change the religion listed on his government-issued ID. He converted to Christianity in 2018, according to USCIRF.

Prosecutors requested the maximum penalty at a hearing on November 3, and his next hearing is December 8, 2026, when he faces up to 25 years in prison or execution. His lawyers, through Alliance Defending Freedom, which represents him, allege he has been tortured and denied adequate medical care in detention; he is held at 10th of Ramadan Prison.

The letter calls his detention “a grave violation of the fundamental human right to religious freedom” and “part of a very troubling crackdown on Christians and other religious minorities within Egypt,” and warns that US assistance to Egypt is conditioned on improvements in the protection of fundamental freedoms. For the lawmakers, the case is a test of whether a man may speak openly about the faith he chose — and live.
