Syria's Christian Patriarchs Demand Protection After an Archbishop Is Bound and Robbed in HomsLos patriarcas cristianos de Siria exigen protección tras el robo y secuestro de un arzobispo en Homs

November 20, 202620 de noviembre de 2026

Three of Syria's senior Christian patriarchs are calling on the country's authorities to protect religious communities after a violent attack against Melkite Greek Catholic Archbishop Jean-Abdo Arbach in Homs. The archbishop was bound, blindfolded and gagged during an armed robbery at his archdiocesan office on November 14. Robbers reportedly took personal belongings, including religious items. The archbishop later confirmed that he was safe.

The attack prompted a joint statement from Melkite Catholic, Greek Orthodox and Syriac Orthodox leaders, issued from Damascus on November 17. The patriarchs wrote with 'grave concern and profound indignation' about a series of 'heinous and recurring attacks' targeting the Syrian people, and Christians in particular, citing other robberies, kidnappings and assaults affecting Christian communities.

Christians in Syria have lived through more than a decade of war, and the fall of the Bashar al-Assad regime has not brought them security. Church leaders say that while the country's new rulers promise protection for minorities, Christians on the ground still face robbery, intimidation and violence — and that the state must show it can keep its word.

The joint statement was striking in itself: Syria's historic churches, divided by centuries of theology and tradition, spoke with one voice. Their unity underscored the gravity of the moment — and the sense among Christian leaders that their communities cannot afford to face these threats separately.

For the Christians of Homs, the attack on their archbishop was personal. Arbach is not a distant figure; he is their shepherd, and the violation of his office was felt as a violation of the whole community. The patriarchs' appeal now rests with Syria's authorities: protect the churches, investigate the attacks, and prove that Christians have a future in the country their ancestors helped build.

Tres de los principales patriarcas cristianos de Siria piden a las autoridades del país que protejan a las comunidades religiosas, tras un violento ataque contra el arzobispo greco-católico melquita Jean-Abdo Arbach en Homs. El ataque motivó una declaración conjunta de los líderes católicos melquitas, ortodoxos griegos y ortodoxos siríacos, emitida desde Damasco el 17 de noviembre. Los patriarcas escribieron con 'grave preocupación e indignación profunda' sobre una serie de ataques 'atroces y recurrentes' contra el pueblo sirio, y en particular contra los cristianos, citando otros robos, secuestros y agresiones que afectan a las comunidades cristianas.

Los cristianos de Siria han vivido más de una década de guerra, y la caída del régimen de Bashar al-Assad no les ha traído seguridad. Los líderes de la iglesia afirman que, aunque los nuevos gobernantes prometen protección para las minorías, los cristianos sobre el terreno siguen enfrentando robos, intimidación y violencia, y que el Estado debe demostrar que puede cumplir su palabra.

La declaración conjunta fue notable en sí misma: las iglesias históricas de Siria, divididas por siglos de teología y tradición, hablaron con una sola voz. Su unidad subrayó la gravedad del momento y la convicción de los líderes cristianos de que sus comunidades no pueden enfrentar estas amenazas por separado.
