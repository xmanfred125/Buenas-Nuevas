International Christian Concern (ICC) released a new report on October 19, 2026, titled "The Terrorism Economy: Islamic State's Operations Against Christians", written by ICC Fellow Daniel St John. The study dissects how the Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP) generates income and directs its campaign against Christians in Afghanistan, Pakistan, Türkiye and Europe.

Rather than focusing on casualty counts, the report analyzes the administrative and financial machinery that makes the violence possible: how the group raises funds, moves money and sustains its operations. Its central argument is that disrupting ISKP's access to the financial system is critical to defeating the group.

The report cites former FATF president Je-Yoon Shin on a simple but powerful point: terrorism requires funding. Following the money, the authors argue, offers a practical path to weakening the group's ability to carry out attacks.

The study also documents how ISIS propaganda equates Western foreign policy with Christianity, and urges followers to attack Christians on holidays such as Christmas. The report is part of a broader ICC series that has previously examined extremist violence against Christians in Nigeria and East Africa.

Pakistan's Supreme Court issued a historic ruling on October 7, 2026: it upheld the death sentence of Christian Qaisar Ayub, 49, for alleged blasphemy, while acquitting his younger brother Amoon Ayub, 46. Both men had spent more than a decade in prison awaiting this decision.

The brothers were accused over an online blog containing material deemed insulting to Muhammad. Qaisar maintains he stopped publishing in 2009 and that another person ran the account under his name. Both were arrested in 2014 and 2015, sentenced to death in 2018 under Section 295-C of Pakistan's blasphemy laws, and had their sentences upheld by the Lahore High Court in 2022.

The American Center for Law and Justice (ACLJ), whose Pakistani affiliate OLA argued the appeal, calls the ruling unprecedented and is urging international action. More than 300,000 people have signed its petition for Qaisar's life, according to the organization.

For Christians in Pakistan and the global church, the split ruling is both a mercy for one brother and a tragedy for the other. Prayer and advocacy for Qaisar Ayub are needed now more than ever, as his legal options narrow and his life remains at stake.

Abba Post · October 18, 2026Abba Post · 18 de octubre de 2026

Belgium's Federal Commission for the Control and Evaluation of Euthanasia has released its report covering 2024–2025, and the numbers are sobering. Euthanasia deaths rose from 2,445 in 2020 — 1.9 percent of all deaths — to 4,487 in 2025, or 4 percent of all deaths: more than doubling in just five years, and up 12.43 percent from 3,991 in 2024.

Perhaps most troubling, 1,117 of the 2025 cases involved people not expected to die naturally in the short term — people who were not terminally ill — up from just 311 in 2020. The report also recorded increases in euthanasia for dementia and psychiatric conditions, and two cases involving minors, one each in 2024 and 2025.

Since Belgium legalized euthanasia in 2002, with 235 cases that first year, more than 42,000 people have died by euthanasia in the country. The trajectory shows how quickly a practice framed as a rare last resort can become routine.

For Christians, these figures are a call to a different vision: that every life, however fragile or burdened, bears the image of God and deserves protection. The church's answer to suffering was never a shortcut out of life, but compassionate care and the hope of Christ within it.

La Comisión Federal de Control y Evaluación de la Eutanasia de Bélgica publicó su informe correspondiente a 2024–2025, y las cifras son sombrías. Las muertes por eutanasia pasaron de 2.445 en 2020 —el 1,9% de todas las muertes— a 4.487 en 2025, el 4% del total: más del doble en solo cinco años, y un 12,43% más que las 3.991 de 2024.

Lo más inquietante: 1.117 de los casos de 2025 correspondieron a personas que no se esperaba que murieran de forma natural en el corto plazo, es decir, que no estaban en fase terminal, frente a apenas 311 en 2020. El informe también registró aumentos de la eutanasia por demencia y por condiciones psiquiátricas, y dos casos de menores, uno en 2024 y otro en 2025.

Desde que Bélgica legalizó la eutanasia en 2002, con 235 casos ese primer año, más de 42.000 personas han muerto por eutanasia en el país. La trayectoria muestra con qué rapidez una práctica presentada como un recurso excepcional puede volverse rutinaria.

Para los cristianos, estas cifras son un llamado a otra visión: que cada vida, por frágil o agobiada que sea, lleva la imagen de Dios y merece protección. La respuesta de la iglesia al sufrimiento nunca fue un atajo para salir de la vida, sino el cuidado compasivo y la esperanza de Cristo dentro de ella.

A new Lifeway Research survey of 1,003 U.S. Protestant pastors finds that 93 percent do not plan to endorse political candidates during worship services ahead of the 2026 midterm elections — even after being told the IRS said in 2025 it would not strip tax-exempt status for pastoral endorsements. Only 5 percent plan to endorse, and 2 percent are unsure. The survey was conducted September 2–24, 2025, with a margin of error of plus or minus 3.3 percentage points.

Pentecostal pastors (13 percent), Baptist pastors (9 percent) and non-denominational pastors (7 percent) were the most likely to endorse, while Lutheran and Methodist pastors stood near 1 percent. African American pastors (11 percent) were more likely to plan endorsements than white pastors (4 percent).

The legal question remains unsettled: a federal judge rejected the IRS settlement in March 2026, and the appeal before the 5th Circuit is still pending.

For many Christians, the finding is encouraging rather than discouraging. It suggests that most pastors prefer to preach the whole counsel of Scripture and let their congregations apply biblical truth to civic life — keeping the pulpit a place of gospel conviction, not campaign endorsements.

Abba Post · October 14, 2026Abba Post · 14 de octubre de 2026

Everett Public Schools in Washington state, a district of about 20,000 students, has settled a federal religious-discrimination lawsuit filed by LifeWise Academy, which offers voluntary off-campus Bible classes to public-school students during the school day under released-time instruction. Under the settlement, the district must issue a public written apology visible on its website for six months, rewrite its policies on religious activities, require board members to undergo religious-liberty training, and pay $275,000 in attorney's fees.

The district had barred LifeWise from community events and flyer displays, required weekly parental reauthorization, and even made students seal their Bibles in envelopes inside their backpacks. Board member Charles Adkins publicly admitted "animus toward LifeWise Academy." Judge Lauren King granted an injunction in April largely favoring LifeWise's claims.

The case was brought by staff member Sarah Sweeny in December 2025, with LifeWise represented by First Liberty Institute and Bryan Cave Leighton Paisner.

For Christians, the outcome is a reminder that religious freedom in education is not a favor granted by officials but a right to be defended. The district's own apology now stands on its website for six months as a public lesson: hostility toward faith in public schools has consequences.

Según el acuerdo, el distrito debe emitir una disculpa pública por escrito visible en su sitio web durante seis meses, reescribir sus políticas sobre actividades religiosas, exigir a los miembros de la junta recibir capacitación en libertad religiosa y pagar $275.000 en honorarios de abogados.

Beneath the shrine of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, archaeologists have found the remains of a 20-foot-wide circular marble monument built around the rock-cut tomb — described as the earliest archaeologically documented monument ever raised around it.Debajo del santuario de la Iglesia del Santo Sepulcro, los arqueólogos hallaron los restos de un monumento circular de mármol de unos 6 metros de ancho construido alrededor de la tumba excavada en la roca, descrito como el monumento más antiguo documentado por la arqueología en torno a ella.

Abba Post · October 16, 2026Abba Post · 16 de octubre de 2026

Archaeologists excavating beneath the edicule — the small shrine at the heart of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem — have uncovered the remains of a 20-foot-wide circular monument of marble built around the rock-cut burial chamber that Christians have identified for some 1,700 years as the tomb of Jesus.

Researchers describe the find as the earliest archaeologically documented monument ever built around the venerated tomb, and say it may echo a structure commissioned in the fourth century by the Emperor Constantine, who first monumentalized the site in the 330s.

The excavation, led by Sapienza University of Rome archaeologist Francesca Romana Stasolla, found that the surrounding rock had been deliberately cut away to create the circular structure. Stasolla noted that the ancient monumentalization was, in her words, "kept to a minimum, in an effort to preserve as far as possible the original appearance of the venerated Tomb."

For historians of the early church, the discovery matters because it pushes the physical evidence of how the first generations of Christians honored this site back to a time within living memory of the apostolic era's grandchildren. The Holy Sepulchre is not a quiet museum piece: it sits above the traditional sites of both the crucifixion and the burial, and every layer of stone tells the story of a church that has guarded this ground through Roman, Byzantine, crusader, Ottoman, and modern hands.

Presented strictly as history, the find does not prove or disprove faith — no stone can do that. But it confirms what the early Christians themselves claimed: that from the very first centuries, believers treated this rocky ground as the place where death was defeated, and built accordingly. For believers today, it is a reminder that the resurrection is not a legend floated in the clouds but an event the early church located in time and place, worthy of marble.

Los arqueólogos que excavan debajo del edículo —el pequeño santuario en el corazón de la Iglesia del Santo Sepulcro en Jerusalén— han descubierto los restos de un monumento circular de mármol de unos seis metros de ancho, construido alrededor de la cámara funeraria excavada en la roca que los cristianos identifican desde hace unos 1.700 años como la tumba de Jesús.

Los investigadores describen el hallazgo como el monumento más antiguo documentado por la arqueología en torno a esa tumba, y afirman que podría reflejar una estructura encargada en el siglo IV por el emperador Constantino, quien monumentalizaría el lugar por primera vez en la década de 330.

La excavación, dirigida por la arqueóloga de la Universidad Sapienza de Roma Francesca Romana Stasolla, reveló que la roca circundante fue tallada deliberadamente para crear la estructura circular. Stasolla señaló que la monumentalización antigua se mantuvo, en sus palabras, «en un mínimo, en un esfuerzo por preservar en lo posible el aspecto original de la venerada Tumba».

Para los historiadores de la iglesia primitiva, el descubrimiento es importante porque acerca la evidencia física de cómo las primeras generaciones de cristianos honraron este sitio a una época dentro de la memoria viva de los nietos de la era apostólica. El Santo Sepulcro no es una pieza de museo silenciosa: se alza sobre los lugares tradicionales de la crucifixión y la sepultura, y cada capa de piedra cuenta la historia de una iglesia que ha custodiado este terreno a través de manos romanas, bizantinas, cruzadas, otomanas y modernas.

Presentado estrictamente como historia, el hallazgo no prueba ni refuta la fe —ninguna piedra puede hacer eso—. Pero confirma lo que los primeros cristianos mismos afirmaron: que desde los primeros siglos, los creyentes trataron este terreno rocoso como el lugar donde la muerte fue vencida, y construyeron en consecuencia. Para los creyentes de hoy, es un recordatorio de que la resurrección no es una leyenda flotando entre nubes, sino un acontecimiento que la iglesia primitiva ubicó en un tiempo y un lugar, digno de mármol.

The UK government has published updated guidance for Relationships, Sex and Health Education (RSHE) in English schools, and it carries significant protections for families of faith. The guidance preserves parents' right to request that their children be withdrawn from some or all sex education, and requires schools to let parents see every material used in lessons.

Schools are told to be sensitive to pupils' religious backgrounds — and schools with a Christian ethos are explicitly permitted to share a biblical perspective on family, relationships, and sex. The Musée Jacquemart-André is presenting "Timeless Tintoretto," the first exhibition in France to offer a full-career panorama of Jacopo Robusti, known as Tintoretto (1518/19–1594), one of the towering figures of 16th-century Venetian painting. The exhibition gathers around forty paintings and drawings from public and private collections in France, Italy, and elsewhere. What makes Tintoretto matter to readers of this paper is not devotion but craft: his dramatic use of light and bold compositions changed what sacred painting could do, and his influence carried straight into 19th-century French painting — Ingres, Delacroix, Manet, and Cézanne all learned from him.

Viewed as art history, the exhibition is a window into an era when the Christian story was the common language of European imagination, and when painters treated Scripture not as decoration but as the deepest drama worth painting.
