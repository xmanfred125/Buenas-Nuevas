Nigerian Christian woman sentenced to 14 years for sharing her faithCondenan a 14 años de prisión a una cristiana nigeriana por compartir su fe

Liyatu Samuel Dawa, wife of evangelist Amos Suleman, from Gwoza, Borno State — a Boko Haram epicentre — has been sentenced to 14 years' imprisonment for sharing her faith with a Muslim woman, according to a report published November 5 by Voice of the Martyrs Canada.

She was convicted under Section 210 of Borno State's penal code, a provision intended to prevent the unlawful removal of a child from parental custody — although everyone involved in the case is an adult. Liyatu testified that she only offered spiritual guidance to the adult Muslim woman, who had voluntarily chosen to convert to Christianity; the convert confirmed her decision was free and without coercion.

An appeal is being prepared on the grounds that the law was misapplied and that the ruling violates Nigeria's constitution, which guarantees freedom to change religion. Because Liyatu is Christian, Nigerian law stipulates that a Sharia court has no jurisdiction over her, raising questions about the legality of the proceedings.

Borno is one of 12 Muslim-majority Nigerian states that have implemented Sharia law, and the Christian population of Gwoza has fallen from about 30 percent to under 5 percent amid militant violence. Her husband Amos is in hiding after death threats from local militants; he was briefly detained in the case and released, and it is unsafe for him to visit his imprisoned wife.
