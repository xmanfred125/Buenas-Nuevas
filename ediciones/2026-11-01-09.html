Colombia Moves to Declare October 31 the National Bible DayColombia avanza para declarar el 31 de octubre Día Nacional de la Biblia

Bogotá, Colombia — Senator Sara Castellanos presented a bill on October 31 to declare October 31 Colombia's National Bible Day. The initiative seeks to highlight the historical, cultural and religious influence of the Scriptures in the nation's life, and it still must pass through legislative debate before it can become law.

The date is no coincidence. October 31 coincides with Halloween and with the commemoration of the Protestant Reformation, when the Bible was placed again at the center of the church's life. Supporters say a National Bible Day would honor the Book that has shaped Colombia's families, churches and public conscience for generations.

The proposal has drawn support from Christian sectors and questions from others about the religious neutrality of the state. That debate is familiar across Latin America, where several countries already recognize October 31 in law — Chile since 2008 and Argentina since 2024 — and where Paraguay is now considering its own evangelical day.

For Colombian churches, the bill is an opportunity regardless of its legislative fate: a public reminder that the Bible is not a relic but a living Word, read in homes, preached in pulpits and carried into schools, hospitals and prisons every day.

Whether Congress approves the bill or not, October 31 already belongs in Colombia's churches to the same celebration as in the rest of the Protestant world: the day the church remembered that it stands, and falls, on the Word of God alone.

Sus partidarios dicen que un Día Nacional de la Biblia honraría el Libro que ha dado forma a las familias, las iglesias y la conciencia pública de Colombia durante generaciones.

La propuesta ha recibido apoyo de sectores cristianos y preguntas de otros sobre la neutralidad religiosa del Estado. Ese debate es familiar en América Latina, donde varios países ya reconocen el 31 de octubre por ley —Chile desde 2008 y Argentina desde 2024— y donde Paraguay ahora considera su propio día evangélico.

Para las iglesias colombianas, el proyecto es una oportunidad cualquiera sea su destino legislativo: un recordatorio público de que la Biblia no es una reliquia sino una Palabra viva, leída en los hogares, predicada en los púlpitos y llevada a escuelas, hospitales y prisiones cada día.

Apruebe o no el Congreso el proyecto, el 31 de octubre ya pertenece en las iglesias de Colombia a la misma celebración que en el resto del mundo protestante: el día en que la iglesia recordó que se sostiene, y cae, solo sobre la Palabra de Dios.
