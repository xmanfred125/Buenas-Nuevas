At the National Hispanic Christian Leadership Conference's 25th Anniversary Summit, held at the Museum of the Bible in Washington, D.C., on October 20, Pastor Marco Garcia issued what attendees described as the most urgent call of the gathering: American churches must launch a "discipleship revolution" before the harvest is lost.

Garcia, senior pastor of The Way World Outreach, a multicampus church with campuses in Arizona, California and Kenya, told thousands of leaders that the crisis is not a lack of seekers. "We must get prepared for the harvest. The problem is not the harvest. We don't have enough workers," he said, according to The Christian Post. He defined the church's assignment as the Great Commission in Matthew 28:16–20.

The pastor argued that strong Sunday preaching has not produced mature believers. "Start a discipleship revolution in our churches, in our personal lives, in America and the world," Garcia urged, calling leaders to mentor new believers one by one instead of measuring success by attendance alone.

The summit, organized by the NHCLC headed by Pastor Samuel Rodriguez, gathered Hispanic evangelical leaders from across the country to mark 25 years of the movement. Garcia's address closed one of the main sessions and was picked up the next day by Crossmap News, which echoed his headline phrase: "Good preaching is not enough."

For Abba Post readers, the message cuts across every denomination: the future of the Church will be built not in crowds, but in conversations — one disciple making another.

Evangelist J John delivered a blunt challenge to British church leaders this week at the Church Resources Exhibition: stop spending nearly everything on yourselves and start spending on the lost. "Research indicates that we spend over 95% of all of our time, energy, money, resources and manpower on worship and wellbeing," the founder of the Philo Trust said. "We spend less than 5% on witness."

The research behind the warning is sobering. A Philo Trust survey of 1,000 evangelical churches found that only 36 had provided any evangelism training during the previous decade. Speaking at the CRE, and reported by Christian Today, J John urged every congregation to run an evangelism training course each year and to equip ordinary members to share their faith.

His central image was that of the shepherd who looks beyond the flock. "I want you to lead the church as if you were an evangelist… it's a mindset that you're not just thinking about the flock, but you're thinking about the fringe and you're thinking about beyond the fringe," he said.

Crossmap News picked up the address the same week, noting that J John's critique landed at a moment when many UK churches are debating how to reverse decades of decline. His prescription was not a program but a posture: every church led, in his words, "as if you were an evangelist."

The statistic — 95 to 5 — will travel far beyond Britain. It asks every congregation, on every continent, an uncomfortable budget question: how much of what we spend is for those who are not yet here?

El evangelista J John lanzó esta semana un desafío directo a los líderes eclesiales británicos en la Church Resources Exhibition: dejen de gastar casi todo en ustedes mismos y empiecen a gastar en los perdidos. "Las investigaciones indican que dedicamos más del 95% de todo nuestro tiempo, energía, dinero, recursos y personal a la adoración y el bienestar", dijo el fundador de Philo Trust. "Dedicamos menos del 5% al testimonio".

La investigación detrás de la advertencia es aleccionadora. En la CRE, y reportado por Christian Today, J John instó a cada congregación a realizar un curso anual de formación evangelística y a equipar a los miembros comunes para compartir su fe.

United Methodist Church Loses More Than 170,000 U.S. Members in 2025La Iglesia Metodista Unida pierde más de 170.000 miembros en EE. UU. en 2025

The UMC's finance agency reports U.S. membership fell 4.36% in 2025 — more than 170,000 members gone — even as professions of faith ticked upward.La agencia financiera metodista informa que la membresía en EE. UU. cayó 4,36% en 2025 — más de 170.000 miembros menos — aunque las profesiones de fe aumentaron.

Abba Post · October 21, 2026Abba Post · 21 de octubre de 2026

The United Methodist Church lost more than 170,000 members in the United States during 2025, according to a new "Statistical Insights" report from the denomination's General Council on Finance and Administration. U.S. membership fell from roughly 3.988 million in 2024 to about 3.814 million — a 4.36% decline in a single year.

The figures, reported this week by The Christian Post and UM News, rest on reports filed by 20,158 U.S. congregations for 2025, down from 21,176 the year before. In-person average weekly worship attendance slipped from about 1.074 million to 1.041 million, and online attendance fell from roughly 1.037 million to 986,000.

A GCFA spokesperson attributed the losses to multiple causes: removals and corrections by charge conference action, withdrawals, transfers to other UMC or other denominations, deaths, and corrections of prior reporting errors. The numbers continue a longer slide: between 2019 and 2023, more than 7,600 mostly theologically conservative churches left the denomination through disaffiliation, according to UM News.

Amid the decline, UM News reported one bright spot — U.S. professions of faith rose — and cited 34.5 million community ministry interactions across the church's programs.

For Protestant observers, the Methodist numbers are another data point in a decades-long realignment of American Christianity: institutions contracting, but expressions of new faith still breaking through the statistical gloom.

La Iglesia Metodista Unida perdió más de 170.000 miembros en Estados Unidos durante 2025, según un nuevo informe de "Estadísticas" del Consejo General de Finanzas y Administración de la denominación. La membresía estadounidense cayó de unos 3,988 millones en 2024 a unos 3,814 millones: una caída del 4,36% en un solo año.

Las cifras, reportadas esta semana por The Christian Post y UM News, se basan en informes presentados por 20.158 congregaciones estadounidenses para 2025, frente a 21.176 el año anterior. La asistencia semanal promedio presencial bajó de unos 1,074 millones a 1,041 millones, y la asistencia en línea cayó de unos 1,037 millones a 986.000.

Un portavoz de la GCFA atribuyó las pérdidas a múltiples causas: bajas y correcciones por acción de la conferencia de cargo, retiros, transferencias a otras iglesias metodistas u otras denominaciones, fallecimientos y correcciones de errores de informes previos. Las cifras continúan una caída más larga: entre 2019 y 2023, más de 7.600 iglesias, en su mayoría teológicamente conservadoras, abandonaron la denominación por desafiliación, según UM News.

En medio del declive, UM News reportó un punto luminoso: las profesiones de fe en EE. UU. aumentaron, y citó 34,5 millones de interacciones de ministerio comunitario en los programas de la iglesia.

Para los observadores protestantes, las cifras metodistas son otro dato en una reorganización de décadas del cristianismo estadounidense: instituciones que se contraen, pero expresiones de nueva fe que aún rompen la penumbra estadística.

Christian Mother Sentenced to 14 Years in Nigeria for Sharing Her FaithMadre cristiana sentenciada a 14 años en Nigeria por compartir su fe

A Borno court gave Christian mother Liyatu Samuel Dawa the maximum 14-year sentence — no fine option — for sharing her faith with a woman who converted freely, in a case critics say violates Nigeria's constitution.Un tribunal de Borno impuso a la madre cristiana Liyatu Samuel Dawa la pena máxima de 14 años —sin opción de multa— por compartir su fe con una mujer que se convirtió libremente, en un caso que según críticos viola la constitución de Nigeria.

Abba Post · October 21, 2026Abba Post · 21 de octubre de 2026

A Borno State High Court has sentenced Christian mother Liyatu Samuel Dawa to 14 years in prison for sharing her faith with an adult Muslim woman who later converted — the maximum penalty under the statute, with no option of a fine.

Judge A. B. Kumdila handed down the sentence under Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code, a provision concerning the deprivation of a parent or guardian of lawful custody of a child. Dawa, from the Gwoza area of northeastern Nigeria, was convicted after she spoke about Christ to a woman who then chose baptism. That woman testified in court that she converted entirely of her own free will — "nobody forced, threatened, cheated or deceived her," according to court accounts cited by multiple outlets.

Before sentencing, Dawa pleaded simply: "I am a mother. I did not intend to cheat the victim. I only advised her. That is all." The plea was rejected. She remains in custody in Maiduguri.

Her husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman, was detained for months in connection with the case before securing bail; he now lives in hiding after death threats from local extremists in Gwoza.

Critics, including Labour Party senatorial candidate Tochukwu Sagacious Ikezue, say the judgment collides with Section 38(1) of Nigeria's 1999 Constitution, which guarantees freedom of thought, conscience and religion — including the right to change and propagate one's religion. The Borno State Government has not publicly commented, while calls grow for Senate and National Judicial Council intervention. The case was first reported by Truth Nigeria on October 14, with a certified copy of the judgment, and carried this week by International Christian Concern and Nigerian dailies.

Fourteen years for a conversation about Jesus: the sentence has become a test case for whether Nigeria's constitution protects Christians in its far north.

Un Tribunal Superior del estado de Borno sentenció a la madre cristiana Liyatu Samuel Dawa a 14 años de prisión por compartir su fe con una mujer musulmana adulta que luego se convirtió: la pena máxima bajo la ley, sin opción de multa.

El juez A. B. Kumdila dictó la sentencia bajo la Sección 210 del Código Penal del estado de Borno, una disposición sobre privar a un padre o tutor de la custodia legal de un menor. Dawa, de la zona de Gwoza en el noreste de Nigeria, fue condenada tras hablar de Cristo a una mujer que luego eligió el bautismo. Esa mujer testificó en el tribunal que se convirtió por completo por su propia voluntad: "nadie la obligó, amenazó, engañó ni estafó", según relatos del juicio citados por varios medios.

Antes de la sentencia, Dawa suplicó simplemente: "Soy madre. No tuve intención de engañar a la víctima. Solo la aconsejé. Eso es todo". La súplica fue rechazada. Permanece bajo custodia en Maiduguri.

Su esposo, el evangelista Amos Suleman, estuvo detenido meses en conexión con el caso antes de obtener libertad bajo fianza; ahora vive escondido tras amenazas de muerte de extremistas locales en Gwoza.

Los críticos, incluido el candidato senatorial del Partido Laborista Tochukwu Sagacious Ikezue, dicen que el fallo choca con la Sección 38(1) de la Constitución de Nigeria de 1999, que garantiza la libertad de pensamiento, conciencia y religión, incluido el derecho a cambiar y propagar la propia religión. El gobierno del estado de Borno no ha comentado públicamente, mientras crecen los llamados a la intervención del Senado y del Consejo Judicial Nacional. El caso fue reportado primero por Truth Nigeria el 14 de octubre, con copia certificada de la sentencia, y recogido esta semana por International Christian Concern y diarios nigerianos.

Catorce años por una conversación sobre Jesús: la sentencia se ha convertido en un caso de prueba sobre si la constitución de Nigeria protege a los cristianos en el extremo norte.

Abba Post · October 21, 2026Abba Post · 21 de octubre de 2026

Katungu Mbaka Darke was abducted around 9 p.m. in July 2022 during an Allied Democratic Forces attack on Lume village in eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. This week, Ugandan People's Defence Forces soldiers rescued her in Ituri Province and returned her to her community — alive, after years in captivity.

During her years with the rebels she endured severe abuse and relentless pressure to abandon Christianity. "They forced me to accept Islam and memorize some Quranic verses. I just accepted, but in my heart, I knew Jesus was God," she said, according to International Christian Concern, which reported her rescue on October 21. She had two children with a rebel while in captivity.

Back in Lume, the toll of the years is written in grief. Village chief Kimbi Tsobo confirmed the abduction and said the family had held a burial ceremony believing she was dead — "we thought we had lost her."

Church leaders in nearby Mutwanga thanked God for her return and asked believers to pray for the others still in captivity.

Her homecoming is a rare bright headline from a region where the ADF has terrorized Christian villages for years: a woman who would not surrender her faith in the dark has walked back into the light.

Katungu Mbaka Darke fue secuestrada alrededor de las 9 p. m. en julio de 2022 durante un ataque de las Fuerzas Democráticas Aliadas (ADF) contra la aldea de Lume, en el este de la República Democrática del Congo. Esta semana, soldados de las Fuerzas de Defensa del Pueblo de Uganda la rescataron en la provincia de Ituri y la devolvieron a su comunidad: viva, tras años en cautiverio.

Durante sus años con los rebeldes sufrió graves abusos y una presión implacable para abandonar el cristianismo. "Me obligaron a aceptar el islam y a memorizar algunos versículos del Corán. Acepté, pero en mi corazón sabía que Jesús era Dios", dijo, según International Christian Concern, que informó sobre su rescate el 21 de octubre. Tuvo dos hijos con un rebelde durante el cautiverio.

De vuelta en Lume, el costo de los años está escrito en duelo. El jefe de la aldea, Kimbi Tsobo, confirmó el secuestro y dijo que la familia había celebrado una ceremonia fúnebre creyéndola muerta: "pensamos que la habíamos perdido".

Los líderes de la iglesia en la cercana Mutwanga dieron gracias a Dios por su regreso y pidieron a los creyentes orar por los demás que aún están en cautiverio.

Su regreso a casa es un raro titular luminoso de una región donde el ADF ha aterrorizado aldeas cristianas durante años: una mujer que no entregó su fe en la oscuridad ha vuelto a caminar hacia la luz.

Under prior law, the opt-out decision was irreversible.

Ministers hold a unique "dual tax status" — treated as self-employed for Social Security purposes — and for decades, a young pastor's conscientious objection to the system locked the door permanently, even when ministry income later proved too small to sustain a retirement. The new law reopens that door.

The bill was authored by Rep. Vince Fong of California, partnered in the Senate by Sen. Katie Britt, and championed by House Ways & Means Chairman Jason Smith of Missouri. It moved with rare bipartisan ease: a 40–0 committee vote, a 350–5 House vote, and unanimous consent in the Senate, according to the Ways & Means release and the White House statement.

Ministry leaders welcomed the signing. GuideStone Financial Resources president Hance Dilbeck and Geneva Benefits Group president Ed Dunnington — organizations that serve Baptist and Presbyterian ministry families — praised the measure as a long-sought protection for pastors' retirement security.

For thousands of ministers who made an irrevocable decision in their twenties, 2029 will bring something rare in Washington: a second chance, written into law.
