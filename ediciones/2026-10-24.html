Seven Killed as Gunmen Open Fire at a Church Funeral in JordanSiete muertos al abrir fuego unos hombres armados en un funeral en una iglesia de Jordania

At least seven people were killed and 28 injured when gunmen attacked mourners at a Greek Orthodox church funeral in Karak on Friday, prompting three days of national mourning and a pledge of the harshest punishment from King Abdullah II.Al menos siete personas murieron y 28 resultaron heridas cuando unos hombres armados atacaron a los asistentes a un funeral en una iglesia ortodoxa griega en Karak el viernes, lo que llevó a tres días de luto nacional y a la promesa del rey Abdalá II del castigo más severo.

Abba Post · October 23, 2026Abba Post · 23 de octubre de 2026

Friday, October 23, brought horror to a funeral in southern Jordan. Gunmen opened fire on mourners gathered at a Greek Orthodox church in Hamoud village, in the Karak governorate about 180 kilometres south of Amman, killing at least seven people and wounding 28 others, according to Jordanian authorities and international reporting. Early casualty counts varied as the day wore on, with some outlets reporting higher injury figures, but the core facts were confirmed by the government within hours.

Jordanian state television broadcast footage appearing to show three armed men approaching the church and opening fire. Security officials said the attackers were unidentified and remained at large as investigations proceeded urgently, and the government said findings on any organizational links would be announced once complete.

King Abdullah II condemned the shooting as a terrorist attack and promised that those responsible would face the harshest penalties, declaring that the blood of Jordanians is precious and that the nation would respond with an iron fist. On Friday evening he visited the injured at Karak Government Hospital. Prime Minister Jafar Hassan ordered three days of national mourning from Friday, with flags at half-mast across ministries and public institutions.

Government spokesman Mohammad Momani attributed the attack to extremist takfiri ideology, a doctrine that brands others as unbelievers, and stressed that Christians, roughly two percent of Jordan's population, are an authentic and indivisible part of the national fabric. United States Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz called the attack devastating and said attacks on Christians worldwide must be stopped.

Jordan is known for peaceful coexistence between faiths, and the last major terrorist incident in Karak came in 2016. That is what makes Friday's attack on a funeral, a moment when families gather to grieve, so shocking. Abba Post joins Christians everywhere in praying for the bereaved, for the wounded, two of them reported in critical condition, and for a church community that buried its dead under the shadow of gunfire.

The predominantly Christian village of Al-Suqaylabiyah is demanding security and justice after armed men from a neighboring village fired on homes and shot at a priest's car on the night of October 3.La aldea mayoritariamente cristiana de Al-Suqaylabiyah exige seguridad y justicia después de que hombres armados de una aldea vecina dispararan contra casas y contra el auto de un sacerdote la noche del 3 de octubre.

Abba Post · October 24, 2026Abba Post · 24 de octubre de 2026

The predominantly Christian village of Al-Suqaylabiyah in Syria is calling for security, justice and accountability after its second attack in six months, according to a report published October 24 by International Christian Concern. The village says young men from the neighboring, predominantly Muslim village of Al-Qalaa first tried to intimidate a shop owner, were driven off by villagers who came to his defense, and then returned armed and in greater numbers on the night of October 3.

That night, the attackers fired at houses and even shot at an Orthodox priest in his vehicle. The priest escaped unharmed. Police responded and arrested several attackers, along with two Christian men who were released two days later. No fatalities were immediately reported, but the trauma, residents say, runs deep.

Father Fadi Sankari, a Greek Orthodox priest in the village, wrote on social media that no one has the right to enter homes or threaten people's safety and dignity, and urged Christians to stay strong: "The Lord is with us, and we will not leave each other."

The village is still recovering from the previous attack, shortly before Easter, when a mob carrying sticks and knives attacked businesses and people after villagers stopped young men harassing a young woman. One eyewitness recalled that when the mob tried to break into a neighbor's shop, the owner shouted that he was Sunni, and they left him alone and vandalized the rest of the property.

Now the community hopes this time will be the last, while preparing for more violence. "We hope this time to be the last time, but reality shows that each attack is worse than the previous," one resident said. Pray for Al-Suqaylabiyah: for protection over its homes, for justice that actually holds, and for a witness that refuses to leave, even when leaving would be easier.

Ukrainian evangelical churches have been honored with the Hope Award of the European Evangelical Alliance at the Hope for Europe 2026 gathering in Golem, Albania, where delegates from more than 30 countries met this month. The award recognizes a church that has kept preaching, baptizing and serving while its country is at war.

Accepting the award, Igor Bandura, general secretary of the Ukraine Council of Protestant Evangelical Churches, thanked evangelical Christians across the continent for their participation in Ukraine's physical and spiritual struggle. "Just yesterday, 22 civilians, including children, were killed in a town north of Ukraine," he said, according to Evangelical Focus. "But God is at work. Churches continue to work. More than 40,000 believers have been baptised since the war started."

Bandura added that 2,000 pastors and deacons have been ordained in the same period. In soon four years of total war, he said, Ukrainians have experienced terrible tragedy and great lessons from God, but the war is not over and the battle continues: "Let's stand together in prayer and support."

As the evening closed, delegates from other European countries wrote prayers and words of encouragement for Ukraine on a wall of prayers, and a Ukrainian delegation stood before it in gratitude. Previous recipients of the award include A Rocha, for caring for God's creation, the Greek Evangelical Alliance, for receiving refugees in 2016, and Home for Good, for welcoming children who had nowhere to go.

The numbers tell a story the headlines miss: bombs have not stopped baptisms, and funerals have not stopped ordinations. In Ukraine, the church is not merely surviving the war. It is growing through it, one baptism at a time.

Abba Post · October 23, 2026Abba Post · 23 de octubre de 2026

Evangelicals in Northern Ireland have expressed clear support for the victims of sexual abuse by one of the region's most influential political leaders, after judges found Jeffrey Donaldson guilty of 18 historical sex offences against two women when they were children, including one count of rape. Donaldson, who led the Democratic Unionist Party between 2021 and 2024 and headed its Westminster parliamentary group from 2019, resigned after being charged. His wife was also convicted of complicity but could not stand trial due to her mental health.

David Smyth, head of the Evangelical Alliance Northern Ireland, said the unanimous verdict on all charges is a clear vindication for the two brave women who testified, identified publicly only as A and B. "Our thoughts and prayers are with them, today and in the days to come," he said.

The Alliance called the moment another sombre occasion on which Christians need to reconsider how they respond better to violence against women and girls. It noted, pointedly, that the public figure who wore a Christian fish pin on his lapel has yet to show signs of remorse or repentance, while the two women demonstrated, in its words, the faith they professed with courage and grace.

Donaldson is a member of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland and openly professed Christian faith, in a region where Catholic and Protestant backgrounds have deeply shaped history and voting patterns. The case therefore cuts close to home for Northern Ireland's churches: the abuser was not an outsider to the faith community, but a man who spoke its language in public life.

For the wider church, the Alliance's response is a model worth noting. It did not circle the wagons, minimize, or hide behind a shared confession. It named the verdict a vindication, centered the victims, and asked the church to learn. Justice, in this case, did not come from the church's platform. It came for two women who told the truth, and the church's job now is to stand with them and make sure it never happens again.

Korean Churches Are Breaking from Tradition to Win Back Their YouthLas iglesias coreanas rompen con la tradición para recuperar a sus jóvenes

A 2026 survey found 26 percent of South Korean university students believe but do not attend church, and 41 percent of churchgoing teens have considered leaving, pushing congregations to trade prohibition-centered faith for personal, relational discipleship.Una encuesta de 2026 halló que el 26 por ciento de los universitarios surcoreanos cree pero no asiste a la iglesia, y que el 41 por ciento de los adolescentes que asisten ha considerado irse, lo que empuja a las congregaciones a cambiar una fe centrada en prohibiciones por un discipulado personal y relacional.

Abba Post · October 23, 2026Abba Post · 23 de octubre de 2026

South Korean Christianity grew at a pace the world marveled at: Christians rose from 18 percent of the population in 1970 to 30 percent in 2000, and the country became the world's second-largest missionary-sending nation, home to the largest megachurch on earth, Yoido Full Gospel Church. But a new report from Christianity Today, drawing on a 2026 survey by the Pastoral Data Research Institute, shows the next generation slipping away.

The numbers are sobering. Twenty-six percent of university students identified as believers but do not attend church. Among teenagers who still attend, 41 percent have considered leaving. Seventy-four percent of those who have already left said they had no one with whom to share their struggles at church. And the most cited reason for leaving was that worship services and sermons feel boring and meaningless.

Pastor Park Chan-yeol of Knock Church, who grew up in a large Pentecostal church, argues the exodus is not caused by a lack of personal faith. He blames a prohibition-centered faith: churches have focused too heavily on what not to do, while failing to explain why belief matters in the first place. "The church should help young people discover and develop a faith of their own," he said.

Some congregations are already experimenting. In Seoul's Hongdae nightlife district, Newsong Church began in 2013 in a rented basement club, holding worship among bars and entertainment venues to show young people that faith can exist in the heart of secular culture. Earlier this year it moved into a larger facility near its original location to fit its growing congregation.

The Korean story is a warning and an invitation for churches everywhere. Programs, polish and prohibition will not hold a generation that longs to be known. What keeps young people, the data suggests, is someone to talk to, a faith that makes sense of their lives, and a church willing to meet them where they actually are, even in a basement club in Hongdae.

El cristianismo surcoreano creció a un ritmo que el mundo admiraba: los cristianos pasaron del 18 por ciento de la población en 1970 al 30 por ciento en 2000, y el país se convirtió en la segunda nación que más misioneros enviaba en el mundo, hogar de la megáiglesia más grande del planeta, la Iglesia del Evangelio Completo de Yoido. Pero un nuevo informe de Christianity Today, basado en una encuesta de 2026 del Instituto de Investigación de Datos Pastorales, muestra que la próxima generación se está alejando.

Las cifras son aleccionadoras. El 26 por ciento de los estudiantes universitarios se identificó como creyente pero no asiste a la iglesia. Entre los adolescentes que aún asisten, el 41 por ciento ha considerado irse. El 74 por ciento de los que ya se fueron dijo que no tenía a nadie con quien compartir sus luchas en la iglesia. Y la razón más citada para irse fue que los cultos y los sermones se sienten aburridos y sin sentido.

El pastor Park Chan-yeol, de la iglesia Knock, quien creció en una gran iglesia pentecostal, sostiene que el éxodo no se debe a falta de fe personal. Culpa a una fe centrada en prohibiciones: las iglesias se han concentrado demasiado en lo que no hay que hacer, sin explicar por qué la fe importa en primer lugar. «La iglesia debería ayudar a los jóvenes a descubrir y desarrollar una fe propia», dijo.

Algunas congregaciones ya están experimentando. En el distrito de vida nocturna de Hongdae, en Seúl, la iglesia Newsong comenzó en 2013 en un club subterráneo alquilado, celebrando cultos entre bares y locales de entretenimiento para mostrar a los jóvenes que la fe puede existir en el corazón de la cultura secular. A principios de este año se mudó a un local más grande cerca de su ubicación original para dar cabida a su congregación en crecimiento.

La historia coreana es una advertencia y una invitación para las iglesias de todo el mundo. Los programas, el brillo y las prohibiciones no retendrán a una generación que anhela ser conocida. Lo que retiene a los jóvenes, según los datos, es alguien con quien hablar, una fe que dé sentido a sus vidas y una iglesia dispuesta a encontrarlos donde realmente están, incluso en un club subterráneo en Hongdae.

Paraguay's Senate Declares the Last Sunday of October as Evangelical Church DayEl Senado de Paraguay declara el último domingo de octubre como Día de la Iglesia Evangélica

Abba Post · October 23, 2026Abba Post · 23 de octubre de 2026

Paraguay's Senate has approved a bill declaring a national Evangelical Church Day, to be commemorated each year on the last Sunday of October. The initiative aims, in its own words, to spread and promote the historical, cultural, social, educational, humanitarian and spiritual contribution of Paraguay's evangelical churches.

The bill was presented on August 12 by senators Orlando Penner, Lizarella Valiente, Blanca Ovelar and Gustavo Leite. In its original version it proposed October 31 of each year as the national day, a date with obvious resonance for Protestants as Reformation Day. During the Senate's ordinary session, the date was amended to the last Sunday of October.

Two expectations around the bill were set aside in the final text. The original proposal did not create a national holiday, and it remains a commemorative date only. And at Senator Blanca Ovelar's motion, the Senate removed an article that would have required the Ministry of Education and Sciences, through its Vice Ministry of Worship, to include the day in the school calendar.

The result is modest but meaningful: a public, national recognition of a community that has long served Paraguay in schools, hospitals, social work and preaching, without turning the commemoration into a state holiday or a curricular obligation.

For Paraguay's evangelicals, the first celebration will fall on a Sunday, in church, where it belongs. A day set apart by law, kept holy by worship: that is a fitting way for a nation to say thank you to its evangelical churches.

El Senado de Paraguay aprobó un proyecto de ley que declara el Día de la Iglesia Evangélica, a conmemorarse cada año el último domingo de octubre. La iniciativa busca, en sus propias palabras, difundir y promover el aporte histórico, cultural, social, educativo, humanitario y espiritual de las iglesias evangélicas del Paraguay.

El proyecto fue presentado el 12 de agosto por los senadores Orlando Penner, Lizarella Valiente, Blanca Ovelar y Gustavo Leite. En su versión original proponía el 31 de octubre de cada año como día nacional, una fecha con obvia resonancia para los protestantes como Día de la Reforma. Durante la sesión ordinaria del Senado, la fecha se modificó al último domingo de octubre.

Dos expectativas en torno al proyecto quedaron fuera del texto final. La propuesta original no creaba un feriado nacional, y sigue siendo solo una fecha conmemorativa. Y a moción de la senadora Blanca Ovelar, el Senado eliminó un artículo que habría obligado al Ministerio de Educación y Ciencias, a través de su Viceministerio de Culto, a incluir el día en el calendario escolar.

El resultado es modesto pero significativo: un reconocimiento público y nacional de una comunidad que hace tiempo sirve a Paraguay en escuelas, hospitales, obra social y predicación, sin convertir la conmemoración en feriado estatal ni en obligación curricular.

Para los evangélicos paraguayos, la primera celebración caerá un domingo, en la iglesia, donde corresponde. Un día apartado por ley, santificado por la adoración: esa es una forma adecuada para que una nación dé las gracias a sus iglesias evangélicas.

Nearly 900 Evangelical Leaders Gather in Poland for the European Leadership ForumCasi 900 líderes evangélicos se reúnen en Polonia para el Foro Europeo de Liderazgo

The European Leadership Forum 2026 brought nearly 900 participants from 45 countries to Wisla, Poland, for Bible teaching, gospel relationships and a frank conversation about weary leaders and religious freedom.El Foro Europeo de Liderazgo 2026 reunió a casi 900 participantes de 45 países en Wisla, Polonia, para enseñanza bíblica, relaciones de evangelio y una conversación franca sobre líderes agotados y libertad religiosa.

Abba Post · October 23, 2026Abba Post · 23 de octubre de 2026

Wisla, a small town on Poland's border with the Czech Republic known for its Protestant heritage, welcomed nearly 900 participants from 45 countries for the European Leadership Forum 2026, an annual gathering now approaching its 25th anniversary and one of the key reference points for evangelical leadership in Europe.

The week combined morning Bible expositions led by pastor Colin Smith, president of Open The Bible, who has ministered in London and Chicago, with afternoon and evening plenary sessions featuring voices as varied as Spanish psychiatrist Pablo Martínez Vila, biblical counselor Helen Thorne-Allenson, British lawyer Dave McIlroy and Australian communicator John Dickson.

The opening plenary set the tone. Martínez asked an uncomfortable question for anyone in ministry: are you tending your own vineyard? Drawing on Song of Songs 1:6, "They made me keeper of the vineyards, but my own vineyard I have not kept," the Barcelona-based psychiatrist described what he called the "empty pool syndrome": the tendency to give without replenishing, to serve without resting, until completely depleted.

Martínez named three common errors in ministry: hyperactivity, because we have not learned to say no, though it is essential for health; spiritual capitalism, an obsession with visible results; and loneliness, which he called one of the greatest enemies of Christian leaders, noting that most moral failures are fed in loneliness. His proposal was as simple as it was demanding: to prune and to water, to renounce and reduce on one hand and learn to be renewed on the other. También permitía a los alumnos entrar en baños y vestuarios del sexo opuesto, permitía a los varones competir en deportes femeninos, y exigía a los alumnos referirse a sus compañeros transidentificados con pronombres del sexo opuesto o enfrentar acusaciones de acoso, intimidación o bullying.

La demanda, presentada el 26 de agosto por la Thomas More Society en nombre de Moms for Liberty, alegaba que la política violaba los derechos parentales bajo la 14.ª Enmienda, así como las cláusulas de Libre Ejercicio y Libertad de Expresión de la Primera Enmienda. Bajo el acuerdo, la junta no volverá a adoptar la política ni ningún derivado de ella, y cada empleado actual y futuro del distrito recibirá una copia del compromiso escrito de la junta de respetar los derechos de los padres.

La directora ejecutiva y cofundadora de Moms for Liberty, Tina Descovich, celebró el acuerdo, diciendo que los padres detrás del caso sufrieron doxxing y acoso simplemente por pedir a su distrito que respetara su papel como educadores principales de sus hijos, y que su perseverancia aseguró una promesa que protege a cada familia de Chathams. El abogado principal de la Thomas More Society, Christopher Ferrara, añadió que los distritos que aún ocultan a los padres las transiciones de los niños pueden seguir el ejemplo de Chathams y cambiar de rumbo ahora, o explicarse ante un juez federal.

La junta ya había derogado y reemplazado todo su manual de políticas y no volvió a adoptar la política de identidad de género bajo el nuevo manual. El acuerdo hace permanente ese cambio: una escuela, dice en efecto el acuerdo, no puede interponerse entre los padres y sus hijos y llamarlo política.

The Gospel is alive and well in California, CBN News reported on October 22, as thousands sought Christ at revival gatherings in the San Diego area. Baptisms on the beach and in city venues drew crowds of new believers and the churches discipling them, in what participants describe as a season of awakening.

The gatherings belong to a wider pattern Abba Post has been following all year: public baptisms, campus worship nights and revival meetings drawing people who might never walk into a Sunday service on their own, but will walk into the ocean with a pastor, a friend and a crowd cheering them on.

Oceanside-area reports frame the moment in deliberately ordinary terms. Prayer at the kitchen table and quiet obedience at work belong to the Christian life just as surely as public worship does, one local account noted, and the most helpful response to a public baptism is not only celebration but discipleship: a church that welcomes people into the water should also welcome them into a life of learning, fellowship and service.

That is the test of every revival, and it is a healthy one. Crowds are easy to gather and hard to keep. Baptism is a beginning, not a finish line, and the churches of San Diego know it: the water is the doorway, and the weeks after it, the walk.

Still, a beginning is worth celebrating. Every baptism, as the gatherings' own refrain puts it, represents a life changed.
