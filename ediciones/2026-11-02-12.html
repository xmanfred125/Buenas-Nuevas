Former NFL coach and outspoken Christian Tony Dungy has stepped into a viral controversy over marriage, answering with Scripture an Ohio pastor who announced he has two wives and called the teaching that a man should have only one wife 'unbiblical.'

Israel Ford, co-founder of LifeWorks Church in the Cleveland area, revealed to his congregation on October 25 that he has been married to two women simultaneously for nearly three years. His first wife, Lydia, introduced church minister Shakayla Ford as his second wife during the service. Ford later defended the arrangement on social media, citing Exodus 21:10 and presenting his decision as obedience to what God had revealed.

Dungy responded on October 27 after another user challenged Christians to produce a single Bible verse explicitly forbidding polygamy. He quoted Ephesians 5:31–32: 'A man leaves his father and mother and is joined to his wife, and the two are united into one. This is a great mystery, but it is an illustration of the way Christ and the church are one.' He added Jesus' words in Mark 10:11, that whoever divorces his wife and marries someone else commits adultery against her.

When a commenter argued that, just as Jesus has many followers, a man can have many wives, Dungy answered: 'But Jesus has only one bride — the church.' He also quoted Ephesians 5:6, warning against being fooled by those who try to excuse such sins. Other Christians joined the exchange, citing 1 Timothy 3:2 and Titus 1:6, which require an overseer to be the husband of one wife.

The controversy has become a teaching moment far beyond one congregation. It asks the church to weigh a claim of personal revelation against the whole counsel of Scripture — from Genesis, where marriage begins as one man and one woman becoming one flesh, to Ephesians, where marriage pictures Christ and his single bride.

Israel Ford, cofundador de LifeWorks Church en el área de Cleveland, reveló a su congregación el 25 de octubre que ha estado casado con dos mujeres simultáneamente durante casi tres años. Su primera esposa, Lydia, presentó a la ministra de la iglesia Shakayla Ford como su segunda esposa durante el culto. Ford defendió después el arreglo en redes sociales, citando Éxodo 21:10 y presentando su decisión como obediencia a lo que Dios le había revelado.

Dungy respondió el 27 de octubre después de que otro usuario desafiara a los cristianos a presentar un solo versículo bíblico que prohibiera explícitamente la poligamia. Citó Efesios 5:31–32: 'El hombre dejará a su padre y a su madre, y se unirá a su mujer, y los dos serán uno solo. Esto es un gran misterio, pero ilustra la manera en que Cristo y la iglesia son uno'. Añadió las palabras de Jesús en Marcos 10:11, que quien se divorcia de su esposa y se casa con otra comete adulterio contra ella.

Cuando un comentarista argumentó que, así como Jesús tiene muchos seguidores, un hombre puede tener muchas esposas, Dungy respondió: 'Pero Jesús tiene una sola novia: la iglesia'. También citó Efesios 5:6, advirtiendo contra dejarse engañar por quienes intentan excusar tales pecados. Otros cristianos se sumaron al intercambio, citando 1 Timoteo 3:2 y Tito 1:6, que exigen que un obispo sea esposo de una sola mujer.

La controversia se ha convertido en un momento de enseñanza mucho más allá de una congregación. Pide a la iglesia sopesar una afirmación de revelación personal frente a todo el consejo de la Escritura, desde Génesis, donde el matrimonio comienza como un hombre y una mujer que llegan a ser una sola carne, hasta Efesios, donde el matrimonio retrata a Cristo y a su única novia.
