A Chinese house-church elder imprisoned for organizing religious gatherings outside the government-approved church system was released on Wednesday after serving a 16-month sentence, according to reports published on November 2.

The elder's release is a rare moment of relief for China's independent churches, which have faced a sustained crackdown in recent years. Authorities have used a nationwide campaign against unregistered organizations to shut down independent churches and religious fellowships, raising concern among believers and advocates.

Reports from the same period describe raids on house churches, orders to replace hymns with songs praising the ruling party, seminaries required to feature military drills and party ideology, and imprisoned pastors denied access to Bibles. Two pastors connected with the elder's movement remain jailed, a reminder that one release does not end the pressure.

House churches — congregations that meet in homes and other informal settings rather than in state-registered churches — have long been at the center of the tension between religious freedom and state control in China. Their leaders often face charges tied to organizing gatherings the authorities have not approved.

Churches abroad are being urged to pray both for the elder's recovery and reintegration and for the two pastors still in prison, and for congregations that must decide each week whether gathering to worship is worth the risk.

La liberación del anciano es un raro momento de alivio para las iglesias independientes de China, que enfrentan una represión sostenida en los últimos años. Las autoridades han usado una campaña nacional contra organizaciones no registradas para cerrar iglesias independientes y comunidades religiosas, lo que genera preocupación entre creyentes y defensores.

Informes del mismo período describen redadas en iglesias caseras, órdenes de reemplazar himnos con canciones que alaban al partido gobernante, seminarios obligados a incluir ejercicios militares e ideología del partido, y pastores encarcelados a quienes se niega el acceso a Biblias. Dos pastores vinculados con el movimiento del anciano siguen en prisión, un recordatorio de que una liberación no pone fin a la presión.

Las iglesias caseras —congregaciones que se reúnen en hogares y otros espacios informales en lugar de iglesias registradas por el Estado— han estado durante mucho tiempo en el centro de la tensión entre la libertad religiosa y el control estatal en China. Sus líderes suelen enfrentar cargos relacionados con organizar reuniones que las autoridades no han aprobado.
