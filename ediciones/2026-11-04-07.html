An Ohio megachurch fired its founding pastor on Tuesday after an investigation concluded that he had engaged in sexual misconduct with a member of staff. The church announced the dismissal of Lonnie Snell, saying the decision followed a careful review of the facts.

The announcement is the latest in a painful series of leadership failures that have shaken large American churches in recent years. Each case leaves behind not only a fallen leader, but a congregation forced to reckon with broken trust and staff members who deserve care, protection and a voice.

Church leaders said the investigation was conducted to establish the truth and to act on it, however difficult the outcome. They asked the congregation for prayer as the church moves toward healing, accountability and the selection of new leadership.

For the wider church, the story is a sober reminder that giftedness is not godliness, and that no ministry, however large or fruitful, is exempt from the biblical call to holiness. Scripture's standard for overseers remains what it has always been: above reproach, self-controlled and faithful in every area of life.

The church now faces the hard work of restoration: caring for those hurt, rebuilding trust from the ground up, and showing a watching world that repentance is real and that Christ, not any pastor, is the foundation of his church.

Para la iglesia en general, la historia es un recordatorio sobrio de que el talento no es piedad, y que ningún ministerio, por grande o fructífero que sea, está exento del llamado bíblico a la santidad. El estándar de la Escritura para los pastores sigue siendo el de siempre: irreprochables, sobrios y fieles en cada área de la vida.

La iglesia enfrenta ahora el difícil trabajo de la restauración: cuidar a los heridos, reconstruir la confianza desde cero y mostrar a un mundo que observa que el arrepentimiento es real y que Cristo, y no ningún pastor, es el fundamento de su iglesia.
