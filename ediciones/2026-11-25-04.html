Alabama Tops Religious Freedom Index; Michigan Ranks LastAlabama, el mejor estado para la libertad religiosa; Michigan, el peor, según el nuevo índice

November 23, 202623 de noviembre de 2026

Alabama is the best state in America for religious freedom, and Michigan is the worst, according to the Napa Legal Institute's fourth annual Faith and Freedom Index, published on November 16 and covered widely this week. The index grades the laws of all 50 states and Washington, D.C., across 15 categories affecting churches, religious schools, pregnancy centers, and nonprofit organizations.

Alabama scored 80% overall, followed by Kansas at 76%, Indiana at 75%, and Texas at 71%. At the bottom were Michigan at 34%, Washington at 38%, and Massachusetts at 41%. More than half of all states — 32 of them — scored below 50%, according to the report.

On religious freedom alone, only Alabama (94%) and Kansas (87%) scored above 80%. Michigan ranked last for the second year in a row. The index also produced surprises: New York, at 55%, outscored conservative states such as Tennessee, North Dakota, and South Carolina, a reminder that religious liberty does not always follow partisan lines.

For churches and ministries, the index is more than a ranking. Its authors say it is a practical tool: a pastor considering where to plant a church, a Christian school weighing an expansion, or a pregnancy center choosing a location can see, state by state, where the law protects their work and where it puts obstacles in their way.

The broader message of the report is sobering. If most states score below 50%, then religious freedom in America depends less on the Constitution than on the zip code. The index invites believers not only to celebrate the states that lead, but to pray and work for the ones at the bottom — and to remember that freedom, once lost, is far harder to recover than to defend.
