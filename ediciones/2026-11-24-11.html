Vanderbilt Rolls 5-0 in NCAA Opener as Defender Says: "I'm Here for Jesus"Vanderbilt golea 5-0 en su debut en la NCAA y su defensora lo tiene claro: «Estoy aquí por Jesús»

Top-seeded Vanderbilt opened the NCAA women's tournament with a 5-0 win over UT Martin, led by a team whose defender Mary Beth McLaughlin says keeps "Jesus at the center of it all."Vanderbilt, cabeza de serie N.º 1, abrió el torneo femenino de la NCAA con una victoria 5-0 sobre UT Martin, liderado por un equipo cuya defensora Mary Beth McLaughlin dice que mantiene «a Jesús en el centro de todo».

November 20, 202620 de noviembre de 2026

Vanderbilt, the No. 1 seed, beat UT Martin 5-0 on Friday, November 20, at the Vanderbilt Soccer Complex in Nashville, in the first round of the NCAA women's tournament, advancing to the second round. The Commodores took 32 shots to UT Martin's two, and three second-half goals in minutes 49, 78 and 82 completed the rout after two first-half goals.

Sydney Watts scored her 41st career goal and 16th of the season; Megan Hinnenkamp scored her eighth of the year; freshman Hailey Perkins scored her first collegiate goal and Mia Herbert closed the 5-0. Vanderbilt entered the tournament at 16-1-1 and is the No. 1 seed for the second straight year.

Behind the scoreline is a team that talks openly about its faith. Defender Mary Beth McLaughlin, a junior from the Atlanta area, has started every Vanderbilt game in the last two seasons and was named to the All-SEC first team. On Sports Spectrum's "What's Up" podcast, she said: "That's what's so different about our team right now… I continuously am like, 'Jesus is at the center of it all.'" The piece's headline said it plainly: "I'm here for Jesus."

McLaughlin's words are not a slogan printed on a warm-up shirt. In the podcast she described a locker room where faith is the shared center, not a private habit — a team identity built around the same confession that shapes her own game.

Vanderbilt's next game is against Wisconsin on Friday, November 27. The College Cup semifinals will be held on December 10 in Cary, North Carolina, with the final on December 13. If the Commodores keep playing the way they did on Friday, they will arrive there as favorites — and, if McLaughlin has her way, with Jesus still at the center of it all.
