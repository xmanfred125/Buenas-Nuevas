PCUSA Pastor's Claim That God Is Trans Sparks Theological BacklashPolémica teológica: pastora presbiteriana dice que Dios es no binario y trans

The Rev. Jacqui Lewis, ordained in the Presbyterian Church (USA) and senior minister of Middle Collegiate Church in New York City, said in a recent talk that God is "queer" and transgender. "If I am a little bit queer, God is very queer... God does not have a penis... God does not have a vagina... God is a 'they,' and that is biblical," she said, according to reports of the talk published on November 23.

Lewis argued from the feminine gender of the Hebrew word "ruach" (spirit) and the plural language of Genesis 1:26, "Let us make man in our image." Christian commentators noted that she also cited the Greek word "pneuma" as feminine, when in fact it is neuter — a mistake that, critics say, undermines the argument built on it.

The clip, posted on X by The Dissenter on November 18, drew nearly 20,000 impressions and a wave of responses. Lewis had already published an essay on Substack in October 2025 titled "God May Be Trans." Evangelist Justin Peters criticized pastor Josh Howerton for having presented Lewis as an example of Christian faith.

Lewis, who graduated with honors from Princeton Theological Seminary in 1992, leads a congregation affiliated with the United Church of Christ. Her defenders say she is describing God's transcendence over human categories; her critics say she is remaking God in the image of a cultural moment.

The debate touches a question older than any headline: how should Christians speak about a God who is spirit, neither male nor female, yet who reveals himself in Scripture as Father and as Son? The Bible is clear that God is not bound by human sex — and equally clear that he has chosen the language by which he wants to be known. That distinction is where the argument will be won or lost.

La Biblia es clara en que Dios no está limitado por el sexo humano, e igualmente clara en que ha elegido el lenguaje con el que quiere ser conocido. Esa distinción es donde se ganará o se perderá el argumento.
