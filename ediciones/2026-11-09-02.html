Pastor killed and more than 40 kidnapped in attack on a church in Ekiti StatePastor asesinado y más de 40 secuestrados en un ataque contra una iglesia en el estado de Ekiti

Five armed men stormed a church in south-west Nigeria during a special evening programme, killing the pastor and abducting at least 40 members of the congregation.Cinco hombres armados irrumpieron en una iglesia del suroeste de Nigeria durante un programa especial nocturno, mataron al pastor y secuestraron al menos a 40 miembros de la congregación.

Abba Post · November 8, 2026Abba Post · 8 de noviembre de 2026

Five armed men stormed the Apostolic Church of Christ in Ilejemeje local government area of Ekiti State, in south-west Nigeria, at about 8:30 p.m. on Sunday, during a special programme. The attackers killed the pastor and abducted at least 40 members of the congregation. As of the latest reports, there is still no word on the whereabouts of the captives.

The attack was confirmed by Ilejemeje local government chairman Pius Alaba and by Ekiti State Police spokesman Sunday Abutu. A joint police and army operation has been launched in response, and the Ekiti state government condemned the attack as 'heinous and unacceptable', deploying a security delegation led by Brig. Gen. Ebenezer Ogundana to the area.

The assault in Ekiti comes just as Nigeria is reeling from an even larger mass kidnapping in Kaduna State, where more than 170 worshippers were taken from three churches on the same Sunday. Together, the two attacks show that no region of the country is safe: the violence that has long scarred the north is now striking congregations in the south-west as well.

The pattern is tragically familiar. Last November, 38 people were abducted from a church in Kwara State. President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has declared a 'national security emergency' and announced the recruitment of thousands of new police officers, but the attacks continue. According to the Observatory for Religious Freedom in Africa, Christians in northern Nigeria are 6.5 times more likely to be killed than Muslims — a statistic that the grieving families of Ekiti now know by experience.
