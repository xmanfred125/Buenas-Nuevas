A group of trans-rights activists protested outside the new Harry Potter flagship store in London on its opening day, Wednesday, November 4. Reports of the size of the group vary — from around six to around 26 people — but the message on their signs was consistent: 'protect trans lives' and 'stop funding trans erasure'. The protesters chanted that author J.K. Rowling has 'blood on her hands', targeting her gender-critical views and her funding of a women's sex-based-rights group.

The store opened anyway. The UK's largest official Harry Potter shop — 21,000 square feet over two floors at The Ribbon, 134-140 Oxford Street — welcomed hundreds of fans on its first day, and no arrests were reported. Rowling recently published an essay saying her views have not changed, and HBO's new Harry Potter television series, with Rowling as executive producer, premieres on December 25, 2026.

For Christians watching the culture wars from the pews, the scene is a familiar one: a beloved story caught between its author's convictions and an audience that no longer agrees on the most basic questions about what it means to be male and female. The protest was small; the argument it represents is not. It is the same argument playing out in schools, in workplaces and in parliaments — and it will not be settled by a demonstration outside a shop.

The argument follows the story into the Christmas season. A Harry Potter-themed Oxford Street Christmas lights switch-on takes place today, November 9, and more than 11,000 people have signed a Good Law Project petition calling for it to be cancelled. The lights, it seems, will go on — and so will the debate.

La tienda abrió de todos modos. La tienda oficial de Harry Potter más grande del Reino Unido —21.000 pies cuadrados en dos pisos en The Ribbon, 134-140 Oxford Street— recibió a cientos de fanáticos en su primer día, y no se reportaron arrestos. Para los cristianos que observan las guerras culturales desde las bancas, la escena es familiar: una historia querida atrapada entre las convicciones de su autora y una audiencia que ya no se pone de acuerdo en las preguntas más básicas sobre lo que significa ser hombre y mujer. La protesta fue pequeña; el argumento que representa no lo es. Es el mismo argumento que se juega en las escuelas, en los lugares de trabajo y en los parlamentos, y no se resolverá con una manifestación frente a una tienda.

El argumento acompaña a la historia hasta la temporada navideña. Un encendido de luces navideñas de Oxford Street con temática de Harry Potter tiene lugar hoy, 9 de noviembre, y más de 11.000 personas han firmado una petición del Good Law Project pidiendo que se cancele. Las luces, al parecer, se encenderán, y el debate también continuará.
