Colton Dixon's 'Bones': A Christian Answer to Halloween from Ezekiel's Valley«Bones» de Colton Dixon: una respuesta cristiana a Halloween desde el valle de Ezequiel

Former American Idol finalist Colton Dixon is putting a faith-filled spin on Halloween with his new single "Bones," inspired by Ezekiel 37, using skeletons and graveyards to proclaim that God brings dead things back to life.El exfinalista de American Idol Colton Dixon da un giro lleno de fe a Halloween con su nuevo sencillo «Bones», inspirado en Ezequiel 37, usando esqueletos y cementerios para proclamar que Dios devuelve la vida a lo muerto.

Abba Post · October 26, 2026Abba Post · 26 de octubre de 2026

Colton Dixon is putting a faith-filled spin on Halloween with his new single, "Bones," using skeletons, graveyards and other spooky imagery to deliver a message about spiritual authority and God's power to bring dead things back to life. The song, released on October 9, was inspired by Ezekiel 37 and represents a passion project he fought to release after sitting on it for more than a year.

In an interview with The Christian Post, the former American Idol finalist said the song was one he personally had to fight for. It is about the Valley of Dry Bones in Ezekiel, he explained, where God tells Ezekiel to prophesy over an army of bones. The lyrics declare: "You bring these bones to life, to life; down in my soul I prophesy; Your fire is the spark in my heart, in my spine; You bring these bones to life, to life."

Rather than shy away from the season's imagery, Dixon has embraced it. The black-and-white music video for "Bones" features a cemetery, skeletal imagery and a gravestone bearing the inscription "Here Lies Colton Dixon." As a kid, Dixon said, he celebrated Halloween hard, but his perspective has changed as his faith has matured.

The song's direction was also shaped by tragedy. In a conversation with CBN, Dixon said the assassination of Charlie Kirk changed his perspective on tackling harder topics through his music, giving him a new urgency about speaking plainly and courageously in a cultural moment that punishes both.

For Christians unsure what to do with Halloween, Dixon offers an answer that is neither retreat nor surrender: take the season's symbols of death and fill them with the gospel's news of resurrection. The bones, in his telling, are not a decoration. They are a sermon.
